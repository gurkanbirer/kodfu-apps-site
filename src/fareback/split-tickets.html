Split tickets and Delay Repay: yes, you can claim

Split ticketing, buying two or more tickets that together cover one journey, is allowed on National Rail. If the train is late, you claim Delay Repay for the whole journey to your destination, sending every ticket. Here is how it works and how to handle a refusal.

Checked 28/11/2026 against the National Rail Conditions of Travel (edition from 30/07/2026) and train company pages.

Split tickets are allowed

The Conditions of Travel (14.2) say you may use a combination of two or more tickets for a journey, as long as the trains you use call at the station where you change from one ticket to the next. You do not have to get off there. A split at a stop where the train only picks up or only sets down passengers is not valid, and some tickets cannot be combined.

How Delay Repay works with split tickets

  • The delay is measured at your final destination, the end of the journey your tickets cover together, not at the split station (Conditions of Travel 32.1 and 33.1).
  • Send every ticket. The Conditions ask for “a Ticket or Tickets” valid for the whole journey. South Western Railway, for example, says you can still claim with split tickets or SplitSave, asks you to say you are claiming for more than one ticket and to upload each one, because it needs to see that they cover the whole route.
  • The amount is worked out ticket by ticket. South Western Railway calculates compensation on the value of each ticket, not the total booking, and adds the results together. Other companies may do it differently; their claim forms show how.
  • Claim once, from the company that caused the delay. If you are not sure which, send it to any company you travelled with: it must pass it on (32.1). Do not claim the same journey from two companies.
  • Use the ticket references, not the retailer’s order number. Several companies, including South Western Railway and Southeastern, refuse third-party order references.
  • Automatic claims may not work. Smartcard and one-click schemes usually need one ticket for the whole journey (Auto Delay Repay on GTR) or cannot handle some split journeys (South Western Railway), so claim by hand.

Example

You travel Leeds to London on two Advance tickets, split at Doncaster, costing £22.00 and £38.00. The train is 40 minutes late into London on a DR30 company. At 50% of each ticket, worked out ticket by ticket: £11.00 + £19.00 = £30.00, the same as 50% of the total here. Claim once, uploading both tickets.

If a split-ticket claim is refused

Some company wording can be read against split tickets. The GTR companies (Southern, Thameslink, Great Northern and Gatwick Express) say missed connections are paid only when enough time was left and you hold a “through” ticket covering the entire journey. Read with Conditions of Travel 14.2, a valid set of split tickets does cover the entire journey. If you are refused:

  1. Reply with every ticket and the planned trains, and point to Conditions of Travel 14.2 (combinations of tickets are valid) and 33.1 (compensation for a journey made on a ticket or tickets valid for the whole journey).
  2. If the company keeps refusing, make a formal complaint and, after its final response or 40 working days, go to the free Rail Ombudsman.

Split ticket questions

Can I claim Delay Repay with split tickets?

Yes. Split tickets are a valid way to travel (National Rail Conditions of Travel 14.2). Claim for the delay at your final destination and upload every ticket.

Is the delay measured at the split station?

No. It is measured at the station where your journey ends.

Do I claim on each ticket separately?

Make one claim for the journey and include every ticket. Some companies, such as South Western Railway, then work out the amount ticket by ticket and add it up.
