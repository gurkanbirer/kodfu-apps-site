Delay Repay and train compensation: questions and answers

Short answers to the questions people ask most about claiming for late and cancelled trains in Great Britain, and about the FareBack app. Each answer links to the full guide.

Checked 28/11/2026 against the National Rail Conditions of Travel (edition from 30/07/2026), the Office of Rail and Road, the Rail Ombudsman and train company pages. General information, not legal advice.

Claiming

How late does a train have to be to claim compensation?

15 minutes at your destination with most train companies (DR15), 30 minutes with others (DR30). Every company must pay from 60 minutes. Check yours in our train company guides.

How much compensation do I get for a delayed train?

On DR15: 25% of a single for 15 to 29 minutes, 50% for 30 to 59 minutes, 100% for 60 minutes or more. Half those shares of a return fare when one way is late, and the whole return from 2 hours. See Delay Repay explained.

How long do I have to claim Delay Repay?

Usually 28 days after the journey. Some companies count from the delay or need the claim to arrive within 28 days.

Who do I claim from?

The train company whose train delayed you, not the website or app that sold the ticket. If you used several companies, any of them must accept the claim and pass it on.

Can I claim if the delay was caused by the weather or someone on the line?

For 60 minutes or more, yes, regardless of fault. For shorter delays most companies pay for any cause, but a company may exclude causes entirely outside the railway’s control; its Passenger’s Charter lists them.

Do I need to keep my ticket?

Keep it, or a clear photo showing the ticket number, journey and price. Many companies refuse receipts and booking emails without a ticket number. Do not let the ticket barrier keep a paper ticket you need.

Does the train company pay automatically?

Sometimes, for tickets bought direct from it, such as Advance tickets or smartcards. Usually you confirm the claim from an email. Check before claiming by hand, because a manual claim can cancel the automatic one.

Tickets

Can I claim on a season ticket?

Yes, per delayed journey. The journey value is the season price divided by a set number, such as 464 for an annual season. See season tickets.

Can I claim with split tickets?

Yes. Split tickets are valid, the delay is measured at your final destination, and you send every ticket. See split tickets.

I missed my connection. Can I claim?

Yes, if you reached your final destination late enough, having left the time the journey planner gave you to change. See missed connections.

My train was cancelled. Refund or compensation?

A full fee-free refund from the retailer if you don’t travel (Advance tickets too), or Delay Repay if you travel later. Not both. See cancelled trains.

What about strikes?

Same as any cancellation for tickets. Season ticket holders can often claim a day back, depending on the company. See strikes.

Does Delay Repay cover the Tube, London Overground or Eurostar?

No. Transport for London (including the Elizabeth line and London Overground), Eurostar and NI Railways have their own schemes.

When things go wrong

My claim was rejected. What can I do?

Ask the company to review it, then complain; companies should give a full response within 20 working days. After a final response or 40 working days, go to the free Rail Ombudsman. See rejected claims, with a letter to copy.

Can I claim for a taxi, hotel or missed flight?

Delay Repay pays up to the ticket price. Other costs are at the company’s discretion, or a claim under the Consumer Rights Act 2015; you cannot be paid twice for the same loss. If you are stranded, ask staff first: companies arrange transport or accommodation where they reasonably can.

Is the Rail Ombudsman free?

Yes, free and independent, and its decision binds the train company if you accept it. See Rail Ombudsman.

The FareBack app

What does FareBack do?

It checks the trains you save against National Rail data after they are due, tells you when a delay means you can claim and roughly how much, and gets your Claim Pack ready: every detail with a copy button and a link to the right claim form.

Does FareBack claim for me or take a cut?

No. You submit every claim yourself on the train company’s website, and any money goes straight to you. FareBack never takes a share.
