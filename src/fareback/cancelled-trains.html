Train cancelled? A refund if you don’t travel, Delay Repay if you do

When your train is cancelled you have two routes, and you pick one. If you decide not to travel, you get a full refund with no admin fee, even on an Advance ticket. If you travel on a later train, you claim Delay Repay for how late you reached your destination.

Checked 28/11/2026 against the National Rail Conditions of Travel (edition from 30/07/2026), National Rail and the Office of Rail and Road.

You cannot have both: once you take a full refund for not travelling, you cannot also claim compensation for the delay (Conditions of Travel 33.5).

If you decide not to travel: a full refund

  • Every ticket type except seasons. If you decide not to travel because your train is cancelled, delayed or rescheduled after you bought the ticket, or your reservation won’t be honoured, you get a full refund with no administration fee (Conditions of Travel 30.1). National Rail says Advance tickets are non-refundable unless your train is delayed or cancelled and you choose not to travel.
  • Turned back? If you set off but cannot complete the journey because of a delay or cancellation and return to where you started, you are also entitled to a refund (30.1).
  • Ask the retailer: the website, app or ticket office you bought from. Delay Repay is the opposite: it goes to the train company.
  • Deadline. The Conditions of Travel (30.2) say to notify the claim within 28 days of the date you intended to travel. Apply within that time.
  • Payment within 14 days of the claim being agreed (30.3), back to the card or account you paid with.
  • Another day instead? For Off-Peak and Anytime tickets, National Rail says there is no admin fee to get a refund and rebook for another day when your planned service was delayed or cancelled. If your train is likely to be more than 60 minutes late, you may also use the ticket on a later date with comparable restrictions (28.4).

Refund rules changed on 01/04/2026 for tickets bought from that date: refunds when you simply change your mind are now only until 23:59 the day before the ticket is valid (admin fee up to £5). Refunds because of disruption are not affected.

If you travel on a later train: Delay Repay

The delay is measured from when the cancelled train was due at your destination to when you actually arrived (against the timetable in force that day). South Western Railway, for example, bases it on catching the next available train from your origin to your destination. Claim for the train you planned to catch.

Example. Your 07:42 is cancelled and the next train arrives 38 minutes after yours was due. On a DR15 company with a £12.40 single, that is 50%: £6.20.

Automatic claims often do not cover cancellations: Northern’s Automated Delay Repay does not apply when the train is cancelled, and South Western Railway says cancelled trains often need a claim by hand. LNER’s One-click is the exception: it is triggered for cancelled trains too.

Season tickets

Seasons are not refunded under condition 30.1; they have their own process. If you don’t travel because of disruption, the train company that caused it may compensate you under its Passenger’s Charter. Many companies let monthly, annual and Flexi holders claim a day back on strike days. See season tickets.

Stranded?

If disruption stops you finishing your journey, train companies will, where they reasonably can, provide alternative transport or, if necessary, overnight accommodation (28.2). Ask staff before arranging a taxi or hotel yourself, and keep receipts. Extra costs beyond the ticket price are at the company’s discretion (32.2) or a Consumer Rights Act claim.

Cancelled train questions

Do I get a refund if my train is cancelled?

Yes, if you decide not to travel: a full refund with no admin fee, for every ticket type except seasons, Advance tickets included. Ask the retailer you bought from, within 28 days of the day you meant to travel.

Can I get a refund and Delay Repay?

No. If you take a full refund for not travelling, you cannot also claim compensation for the delay. If you travel later, claim Delay Repay instead.

My train was cancelled and I took the next one. How much do I get?

Delay Repay on how late you arrived compared with your cancelled train’s arrival time: 25% of a single from 15 minutes on DR15 companies, 50% from 30 minutes and 100% from 60 minutes.
