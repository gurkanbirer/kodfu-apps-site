Rail Ombudsman: when and how to take your claim further

If a train company won’t fix a rejected or underpaid claim, the Rail Ombudsman can decide. It is free, independent and, if you accept its decision, the train company must follow it. You must complain to the train company first.

Checked 28/11/2026 on the Rail Ombudsman’s own site and the Office of Rail and Road. FareBack is not affiliated with the Rail Ombudsman.

When you can go

  • After complaining to the train company. The Ombudsman only takes cases the company has had the chance to resolve.
  • When the company has not resolved it within 40 working days of receiving your complaint, or you are unhappy with its final response, sometimes called a deadlock letter (it can be a letter, an email or even a text).
  • Within 12 months of the company’s final response, unless there are exceptional circumstances.
  • You must be 16 or over, or have a representative who is.

Train companies must tell you about the Ombudsman: their complaint acknowledgements mention it, and they must send you a letter about it when the complaint is deadlocked or the waiting time has passed.

What it covers, and what it doesn’t

Covers: train delays and cancellations, ticket retailing and refunds, customer service, overcrowding and safety, information about journeys or engineering works, passenger assistance and accessibility, station facilities and on-train services, in England, Wales and Scotland.

Does not cover: strike action itself, transport policy or how the industry is run, penalty fares or parking fines, cases already dealt with by a court, issues outside the company’s control, cases where you already accepted the company’s decision or offer, business losses, or claims of more than £2,500.

Is the decision binding?

On the train company, yes, if you accept it. On you, no: if you reject the decision you can still go to court. The service is free for passengers; the industry funds it. ORR sponsors the scheme, which is run by the Dispute Resolution Ombudsman.

How to apply

  1. Gather the train company’s final response (or proof of when it received your complaint), your claim, ticket and evidence.
  2. Apply online through the Rail Ombudsman website, by phone on 0330 094 0362 (Monday to Friday, 09:00 to 17:00), by email to info@railombudsman.org or by post to FREEPOST – RAIL OMBUDSMAN.
  3. Explain what went wrong, what the company said and what you want: for example, the Delay Repay amount due under its scheme.

Rail Ombudsman questions

Is the Rail Ombudsman free?

Yes. It is free for passengers and independent of the train companies.

Can the Rail Ombudsman deal with a rejected Delay Repay claim?

Yes, delays and cancellations are covered, once you have complained to the train company and had its final response or waited 40 working days. It cannot help if you already accepted the company’s offer as a settlement.

How long do I have to go to the Rail Ombudsman?

12 months from the train company’s final response, unless there are exceptional circumstances.
