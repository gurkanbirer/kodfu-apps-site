Lifting operations: free HS&E test practice questions

Planned lifts, slingers and signallers, lifting accessories, thorough examinations and exclusion zones.

6 original questions from the HiVis app for the CITB HS&E Operatives test, each with the answer and an explanation. No sign-up, nothing saved. These are not the real test questions: no app or website can show you those.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    Under LOLER, who must plan every lifting operation?

    1. The crane operator
    2. The person ordering it
    3. A competent person
    4. The site manager's deputy
    Show the answer

    Answer C: A competent person

    Lifts go wrong when the load weight, ground, crane position or slinging are not thought through. LOLER requires every lift to be planned by a competent person, properly supervised and carried out safely.

    Source: LOLER 1998 reg 8

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    What does the safe working load (SWL) marked on a sling tell you?

    1. The weight of the sling itself
    2. The most it is designed to lift safely
    3. The load at which it will break
    4. The weight it can lift only in good weather
    Show the answer

    Answer B: The most it is designed to lift safely

    The SWL or WLL is the maximum load the sling or equipment is designed to lift safely. It is not the breaking load and must never be exceeded.

    Source: LOLER 1998 reg 7

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    During a crane lift you notice a worker about to walk underneath the load. You are not the appointed signaller.

    What should you do?

    1. Give the emergency stop signal and warn the worker
    2. Leave it to the signaller, as only they can signal
    3. Take a photo to report the worker later
    4. Walk under the load to guide the worker out
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Give the emergency stop signal and warn the worker

    Anyone who sees danger may give an emergency stop. Stopping the lift and warning the worker could prevent a death.

    Source: BS 7121

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    During your pre-use check you find a wire rope sling with several broken wires and a sharp kink.

    What should you do?

    1. Use it for lighter loads only
    2. Straighten the kink with a hammer and use it
    3. Take it out of use, label it and report it
    4. Use it but stand well clear during the lift
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Take it out of use, label it and report it

    Broken wires and kinks weaken a sling, which could fail during a lift. Remove it from use and label it so nobody else uses it.

    Source: LOLER 1998 reg 9 and LEEA guidance on discard criteria

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    Which of these is a lifting accessory rather than lifting equipment?

    1. A chain sling
    2. A tower crane
    3. A passenger hoist
    4. A telehandler
    Show the answer

    Answer A: A chain sling

    Accessories, such as slings, chains, shackles and eyebolts, connect the load to the lifting machine. Cranes, hoists and telehandlers are lifting equipment.

    Source: LOLER 1998 reg 2

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    Why must you never tie a knot in a chain or sling to shorten it?

    1. A knot can weaken it so it fails
    2. A knot makes the load hang too low
    3. The knot is hard to undo afterwards
    4. It wears out the crane's hook faster
    Show the answer

    Answer A: A knot can weaken it so it fails

    Knots bend links, wires or fibres far more sharply than they are designed for, greatly cutting the safe working load. Use the right length of sling or a proper shortening device instead.

    Source: LOLER 1998 and LEEA guidance

Revision notes

Planned lifts

The Lifting Operations and Lifting Equipment Regulations 1998 (LOLER) cover lifting equipment such as cranes, hoists, telehandlers used for lifting and excavators used as cranes, and lifting accessories such as slings, chains, shackles and eyebolts. Every lift must be planned by a competent person, properly supervised and carried out safely. Bigger or more complex lifts follow a written lift plan that everyone involved is briefed on.

Thorough examination and checks

A competent person must thoroughly examine lifting equipment before it is first used, at least every 6 months for lifting accessories and equipment that lifts people, and at least every 12 months for other lifting equipment, unless a written examination scheme says otherwise. Equipment is also examined after anything that could affect its safety. Many sites use coloured tags to show accessories are in date. Users carry out a quick check before each use.

Safe working loads

Lifting equipment and accessories must be clearly marked with their safe working load (SWL) or working load limit (WLL), the most they are designed to lift. Never exceed it and never use unmarked accessories. With multi-leg slings, the wider the angle between the legs, the less the sling can safely lift, so the load chart for the angle must be checked.

Slingers and signallers

Loads must be slung and signalled by a trained, competent slinger or signaller. Only one person, the appointed signaller, should give directions to the crane operator, using agreed hand signals or radio, and needs a clear view of the load or a clear radio link. Any person may give an emergency stop signal if they see danger. The operator must not lift if the signals are unclear.

Safe lifting practice

Never stand or walk under a suspended load and keep out of the exclusion zone. Make sure the load is secure, balanced and will not slip before lifting it clear. Use tag lines to control swinging or spinning from a safe distance. Land loads on timber bearers so slings can be removed without trapping hands. Stop lifting if the wind rises above the limit in the lift plan or if visibility becomes poor.

Defective accessories

Check slings and accessories before every lift. Take them out of use if you find cuts, burns or broken stitching on textile slings, broken wires, kinks or crushing on wire ropes, stretched, bent or worn chain links, or hooks that are opened out or missing their safety catches. Never knot a chain or sling to shorten it, protect slings from sharp edges, and store accessories on racks, clean and dry.
