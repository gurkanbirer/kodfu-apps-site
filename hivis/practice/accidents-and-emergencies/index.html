Accidents, first aid and emergencies: free HS&E test practice questions

Reporting accidents and near misses, RIDDOR, first aid and what to do when the alarm goes.

6 original questions from the HiVis app for the CITB HS&E Operatives test, each with the answer and an explanation. No sign-up, nothing saved. These are not the real test questions: no app or website can show you those.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    Why should near misses be reported?

    1. So the person involved can be disciplined
    2. So the cause can be fixed before anyone is hurt
    3. So the site can make a claim on its insurance policy
    4. So the HSE can fine the company for it
    Show the answer

    Answer B: So the cause can be fixed before anyone is hurt

    A near miss is a warning. Reporting it lets the cause be found and put right before it leads to an injury.

    Source: HSE guidance on investigating accidents

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    Who is normally responsible for reporting an incident under RIDDOR?

    1. The worker who was injured
    2. The first aider who treated the injured person
    3. The employer or person in control
    4. The injured worker's own GP
    Show the answer

    Answer C: The employer or person in control

    RIDDOR places the duty on the "responsible person", usually the employer, or the person in control of the premises for some events.

    Source: RIDDOR 2013 reg 3

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    How can you find out who the first aiders are on a new site?

    1. From the site induction and notices on site
    2. By asking the drivers who deliver to site
    3. By looking for whoever wears a white helmet
    4. You cannot, as that information is private
    Show the answer

    Answer A: From the site induction and notices on site

    The induction should tell you about first-aid arrangements, and notices usually show the first aiders' names and where to find kits.

    Source: First-Aid Regulations 1981 reg 4

  4. Question 4 · Select TWO answers

    Which TWO events should site emergency procedures cover? Select TWO.

    1. Fire on site
    2. A late delivery
    3. A trapped worker
    4. Rain on site
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Fire on site, C: A trapped worker

    Emergency procedures deal with situations that put lives at risk, such as fire, collapse or someone needing rescue from height or a confined space.

    Source: CDM 2015 reg 30

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    An injury keeps a worker off normal duties for five days. What does RIDDOR require?

    1. It must be recorded, but not reported
    2. It must be reported to HSE within 24 hours
    3. It must be reported within 15 days
    4. Nothing, unless the worker asks for it
    Show the answer

    Answer A: It must be recorded, but not reported

    Over-three-day injuries must be recorded so patterns can be spotted, but only injuries keeping someone off for more than seven days, not counting the day of the accident, must be reported. Five days falls between the two.

    Source: RIDDOR 2013 reg 12 and reg 4(2)

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    On a small, low-risk site, what does an appointed person for first aid do?

    1. Looks after the kit and calls for help
    2. Gives all medical treatment on site
    3. Runs first-aid training for the site
    4. Signs off workers who are fit to work
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Looks after the kit and calls for help

    An appointed person keeps the first-aid kit stocked and takes charge in an emergency, including calling the emergency services. They are not a trained first aider, so they only give first aid they are trained for.

    Source: HSE L74 First aid at work

Revision notes

Reporting accidents and near misses

Report every accident, injury and near miss to your supervisor, however minor it seems. A near miss is an event that could have caused harm but did not, such as a falling object that misses someone. Near-miss reports show where things are going wrong so they can be fixed before somebody is hurt. Reporting is about learning and preventing a repeat, not about blaming the person who reports.

The accident book

Injuries at work should be recorded, usually in an accident book. An entry gives the date, time and place, what happened and the injury. Accident book pages are designed so each entry can be removed and stored securely, keeping personal details private under data protection law. A proper record also helps if you need to make a claim or the injury turns out to be more serious than first thought.

RIDDOR

The Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR) require the employer or person in control to report certain events to the HSE. They include deaths, specified injuries such as most fractures, amputations and serious burns, injuries that keep someone off normal work for more than seven days, some work-related diseases, and dangerous occurrences such as a scaffold over 5 metres collapsing. Deaths and specified injuries are reported without delay.

First aid

Employers must provide a first-aid kit and enough trained first aiders or appointed persons for the risks and size of the site. Your induction and site notices tell you who they are and where the kits are. In an accident, do not move a casualty unless they are in danger, call the first aider and call 999 for serious injuries. Medicines and tablets should not be kept in first-aid kits.

Emergency procedures

Every site needs emergency procedures for events such as fire, collapse, someone trapped or needing rescue, gas leaks, chemical releases and flooding. Make sure you know what the alarm sounds like, your escape routes and the assembly point. Emergency routes must be kept clear and the emergency services must be able to get in. Drills check the arrangements actually work.

After an accident

After an accident, make the area safe if you can, get help, and leave the scene as it is unless something must be moved to rescue someone or prevent further harm. Give an honest account of what you saw. A good investigation looks for the underlying causes, such as poor planning, equipment or training, so that changes can be made and it does not happen again.

RIDDOR time limits and records

For deaths and specified injuries, the responsible person must tell the enforcing authority without delay and make sure a full report arrives within 10 days. Over-seven-day injuries are reported within 15 days of the accident. An injury that keeps someone off normal work for more than three days must be recorded, for example in the accident book, but does not need reporting. RIDDOR records are kept for at least three years. Dangerous occurrences, such as a crane overturning or plant touching an overhead line, are reportable even if nobody is hurt.
