Asbestos: free HS&E test practice questions

Where asbestos is found, why it kills, and what to do if you think you have disturbed it.

6 original questions from the HiVis app for the CITB HS&E Operatives test, each with the answer and an explanation. No sign-up, nothing saved. These are not the real test questions: no app or website can show you those.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    Which buildings may contain asbestos?

    1. Only factories that were built before 1950
    2. Only buildings that have cement roof sheets
    3. Any building built or refurbished before 2000
    4. Only buildings in poor repair or falling down
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Any building built or refurbished before 2000

    Asbestos was used in a huge range of products until it was fully banned in 1999, so any building built or refurbished before 2000 may contain it.

    Source: Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    How long after breathing in asbestos fibres do asbestos-related diseases usually appear?

    1. Within a few days of the exposure
    2. Within about a year of the exposure
    3. Usually between 15 and 60 years later
    4. Only once the person reaches retirement age
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Usually between 15 and 60 years later

    Asbestos diseases develop slowly, usually 15 to 60 years after exposure, so damage done now may not show for decades.

    Source: HSE asbestos guidance

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    What type of asbestos survey is needed before a building is refurbished or demolished?

    1. A quick visual walk-round by the site manager
    2. A management survey of the whole building
    3. A refurbishment and demolition survey
    4. A survey that covers only the roof space
    Show the answer

    Answer C: A refurbishment and demolition survey

    A refurbishment and demolition survey is intrusive and covers the areas to be worked on, so hidden asbestos is found before the work starts.

    Source: HSE HSG264 Asbestos

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    Who needs asbestos awareness training?

    1. Only licensed asbestos removal workers and supervisors
    2. Only site managers and surveyors
    3. Anyone whose work could disturb asbestos
    4. Only workers aged over 40 years old
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Anyone whose work could disturb asbestos

    Trades such as electricians, plumbers, joiners and labourers can disturb asbestos without knowing. Awareness training helps them recognise and avoid it.

    Source: Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012 reg 10

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    Which buildings does the legal duty to manage asbestos apply to?

    1. Non-domestic buildings, such as offices
    2. Only buildings owned by a local council
    3. Only buildings put up before 1970
    4. Only private homes rented to tenants
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Non-domestic buildings, such as offices

    The duty to manage covers non-domestic premises, including the common parts of blocks of flats. The dutyholder must find any asbestos, record it and manage it, so trades can be warned before they disturb it.

    Source: Control of Asbestos Regulations 2012 reg 4

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    During an induction walk-round you see an old cement panel labelled as containing asbestos. It is in good condition and not in your work area.

    What should you do?

    1. Leave it alone and keep to your work area
    2. Break off a piece to show the site manager
    3. Paint over the label so it is less alarming
    4. Take it down and put it in the general skip
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Leave it alone and keep to your work area

    Asbestos in good condition gives off few fibres if it is left undisturbed, so it is often safer to label and manage it. The danger comes from damaging it, so never touch, break or remove it yourself.

    Source: HSE: Managing asbestos in buildings

Revision notes

Where asbestos is found

Asbestos is a natural mineral fibre that was used widely in buildings until it was fully banned in the UK in 1999. Any building built or refurbished before 2000 may contain it. Common places include sprayed coatings on steelwork, pipe and boiler lagging, insulating board in ceilings, partitions and fire doors, textured wall and ceiling coatings, floor tiles, cement roof sheets, gutters and flue pipes, water tanks, toilet cisterns and roofing felt.

Types of asbestos

The three main types used in the UK were chrysotile (white), amosite (brown) and crocidolite (blue). All of them are dangerous. You cannot tell whether a material contains asbestos, or which type, by looking at its colour: once painted or dirty, it looks like many other materials. Only laboratory analysis of a sample can confirm it, so suspect materials must be treated as asbestos until proved otherwise.

How asbestos harms you

Asbestos is dangerous when it is disturbed and fibres get into the air and are breathed in. It causes mesothelioma (a cancer of the lining of the lungs or abdomen), asbestos-related lung cancer, asbestosis (scarring of the lungs) and pleural thickening. These diseases usually appear 15 to 60 years after exposure and there is no cure for mesothelioma. Around 5,000 people a year in Great Britain die from asbestos-related diseases. Smoking greatly increases the lung cancer risk.

Surveys and the duty to manage

Whoever is responsible for maintaining a non-domestic building must find out whether it contains asbestos, keep a record of where it is and its condition (an asbestos register) and manage the risk. Before refurbishment or demolition, a refurbishment and demolition survey is needed for the area of work. Before you drill, cut or remove anything in an older building, ask to see the asbestos information for that area.

Licensed and non-licensed work

Most work with higher-risk asbestos, such as sprayed coatings, pipe lagging and most insulating board, must be done by an HSE-licensed contractor. Some lower-risk work can be done without a licence, but still needs trained workers, wetting, suitable RPE, disposable coveralls, a special H-class vacuum and proper waste disposal in sealed, labelled bags. Anyone whose work could disturb asbestos must have asbestos awareness training.

If you suspect asbestos

If you think you have found or disturbed asbestos, stop work straight away. Do not try to clean it up, sweep it or use an ordinary vacuum cleaner. Keep other people out of the area and tell your supervisor. If dust has got onto your clothing, do not brush it off or take the clothing home: follow your supervisor's instructions for decontamination and disposal.
