Noise and vibration: free HS&E test practice questions

Noise action values, hearing protection, hand-arm vibration and how to cut exposure.

6 original questions from the HiVis app for the CITB HS&E Operatives test, each with the answer and an explanation. No sign-up, nothing saved. These are not the real test questions: no app or website can show you those.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    Can hearing loss caused by noise at work be cured?

    1. Yes, with hearing aids
    2. Yes, if you rest your ears for a month
    3. No, the damage is permanent
    4. No, but it heals itself after retirement
    Show the answer

    Answer C: No, the damage is permanent

    Noise destroys hair cells in the inner ear and they do not grow back. Hearing aids can help but do not restore normal hearing.

    Source: HSE noise guidance

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    An increase of 3 dB in noise level has roughly what effect on the noise energy reaching your ears?

    1. It makes almost no difference
    2. It increases it by about 3%
    3. It roughly doubles it
    4. It increases it about ten times
    Show the answer

    Answer C: It roughly doubles it

    Decibels are on a logarithmic scale. A 3 dB increase roughly doubles the noise energy, so small changes in level matter a lot.

    Source: HSE noise guidance

  3. Question 3 · Select TWO answers

    Which TWO are symptoms of hand-arm vibration syndrome? Select TWO.

    1. Tingling or numb fingers
    2. Ringing in the ears
    3. Fingers going white
    4. An itchy forearm rash
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Tingling or numb fingers, C: Fingers going white

    HAVS damages nerves and blood vessels in the hands, causing tingling, numbness and fingers turning white, especially in the cold.

    Source: HSE INDG175 Control the risks from hand-arm vibration

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    For the past few weeks your fingertips have tingled for a while after each day using the breaker.

    What should you do?

    1. Ignore it, as it always wears off
    2. Grip the breaker harder to control it better
    3. Report it to your supervisor so it can be checked
    4. Use the breaker for longer spells to toughen up
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Report it to your supervisor so it can be checked

    Tingling after using vibrating tools can be an early sign of HAVS. Reporting it early allows checks and changes before permanent damage.

    Source: Control of Vibration at Work Regulations 2005 reg 7

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    What is the exposure limit value for peak (sudden) noise under the Control of Noise at Work Regulations?

    1. 130 dB(C)
    2. 135 dB(C)
    3. 137 dB(C)
    4. 140 dB(C)
    Show the answer

    Answer D: 140 dB(C)

    Very loud impulse noise, such as from cartridge tools, can damage hearing instantly. 135 and 137 dB(C) are the peak action values; 140 dB(C) is the limit, allowing for hearing protection, and must never be exceeded.

    Source: Control of Noise at Work Regulations 2005 reg 4

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    Why does HSE advise people who use vibrating tools to cut down or stop smoking?

    1. Smoking reduces blood flow to the hands
    2. Smoke makes the tools vibrate much more
    3. Smoking breaks are not allowed on site
    4. Smoke can block the tool's air vents
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Smoking reduces blood flow to the hands

    HAVS damages the blood vessels and nerves in the hands. Smoking also narrows blood vessels and reduces blood flow, which adds to the risk of vibration white finger.

    Source: HSE: Hand-arm vibration (INDG296)

Revision notes

Noise damages hearing for good

Too much noise damages the tiny hair cells in your inner ear. The damage builds up gradually and cannot be repaired, so the hearing loss is permanent. You may also develop tinnitus, a constant ringing or buzzing in the ears. A single very loud bang, such as from a cartridge tool, can cause instant damage. Many people only notice the loss when it is too late.

Noise action values

The Control of Noise at Work Regulations 2005 set levels for daily or weekly exposure. At the lower exposure action value of 80 dB(A), employers must assess the risk, give information and training and make hearing protection available on request. At the upper exposure action value of 85 dB(A), they must reduce noise, mark hearing protection zones and make sure protection is worn. The exposure limit of 87 dB(A), allowing for protection, must not be exceeded.

Judging noise and decibels

Decibels work on a scale where an increase of 3 dB roughly doubles the noise energy, so 88 dB(A) is about twice as damaging as 85 dB(A). A rough guide: if you have to shout to be heard by someone about 2 metres away, the noise is high enough to need assessing. Breakers, disc cutters, piling and compressors are common loud sources on site.

Hearing protection

Hearing protection comes as earplugs or ear defenders. It must suit the noise and fit with your other PPE. It must be worn the whole time you are in the noisy area, because taking it off even for short spells cuts the protection a lot. Insert earplugs properly with clean hands, keep defender seals clean and undamaged, and replace protection that is lost, dirty or damaged.

Hand-arm vibration

Regular use of vibrating tools, such as breakers, hammer drills, grinders, compactors and needle guns, can cause hand-arm vibration syndrome (HAVS). Symptoms include tingling and numbness in the fingers, fingers going white in the cold, pain and loss of grip strength. It can also cause carpal tunnel syndrome. The damage is permanent. Report early symptoms so action can be taken before it gets worse.

Vibration limits and controls

The Control of Vibration at Work Regulations 2005 set a hand-arm exposure action value of 2.5 m/s² A(8) and a limit value of 5 m/s² A(8). Controls include using other methods, choosing low-vibration tools, keeping tools maintained and bits sharp, limiting trigger time and recording it, sharing out the work, and keeping your hands warm and dry. Health surveillance checks workers at risk.

Whole-body vibration

Driving plant such as dumpers and excavators over rough ground can expose the whole body to vibration and shocks, which can cause back pain. The whole-body exposure action value is 0.5 m/s² A(8) and the limit value is 1.15 m/s² A(8). Keep haul roads well maintained, drive at suitable speeds, and adjust the suspension seat to your weight.

Sudden loud noise and hearing checks

The Noise Regulations also set levels for sudden peak noise, such as from cartridge tools, piling or hammering steel: 135 dB(C) is the lower action value, 137 dB(C) the upper action value and 140 dB(C) the limit. When the 87 dB(A) and 140 dB(C) limits are checked, the protection given by hearing protection is taken into account; for the action values it is not. Workers regularly exposed above the upper action values, or otherwise at risk, need regular hearing checks.
