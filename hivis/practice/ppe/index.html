PPE: free HS&E test practice questions

Choosing, wearing, storing and reporting faults with personal protective equipment.

6 original questions from the HiVis app for the CITB HS&E Operatives test, each with the answer and an explanation. No sign-up, nothing saved. These are not the real test questions: no app or website can show you those.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    Where does PPE come in the order of ways to control a risk?

    1. It is the last resort, after other controls
    2. It is the first thing to consider
    3. It replaces the need for a risk assessment
    4. It is only needed for visitors to the site
    Show the answer

    Answer A: It is the last resort, after other controls

    PPE protects only the wearer and only if worn correctly, so other controls such as removing the hazard, guarding or extraction come first. PPE covers the risk that remains.

    Source: PPE at Work Regulations 1992 and Management Regulations 1999

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    Which of these could weaken a safety helmet?

    1. Wiping it clean with a damp cloth
    2. Adjusting the headband so it fits you
    3. Painting it or using a solvent on it
    4. Wearing it every working day
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Painting it or using a solvent on it

    Paint, solvents and some adhesives can attack the plastic shell. Only use cleaning methods and labels that the manufacturer approves.

    Source: Manufacturers' instructions for industrial safety helmets

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    At the start of your shift you notice that your hi-vis vest is badly torn and so dirty that it looks grey.

    What should you do?

    1. Wear it anyway, as some of the colour still shows through
    2. Wear your jacket over it to hide the tear
    3. Ask for a replacement before you go onto site
    4. Only work in areas with no vehicles today
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Ask for a replacement before you go onto site

    Hi-vis only works if it can be seen clearly. A torn, dirty vest makes you harder for drivers and operators to spot, so get it replaced before you start.

    Source: PPE at Work Regulations 1992

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    The gloves you have been given are far too big. You keep dropping fixings and cannot grip your tools properly.

    What should you do?

    1. Carry on without gloves for this job
    2. Cut the fingertips off so you can grip
    3. Tell your supervisor and ask for gloves that fit
    4. Borrow a pair from a workmate for the day
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Tell your supervisor and ask for gloves that fit

    PPE must fit to protect you, and badly fitting gloves create new risks. Your employer should supply the right size, so report it rather than going without or altering them.

    Source: PPE at Work Regulations 1992 reg 4

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    Which eye protection is best against splashes of liquid chemicals?

    1. Chemical splash goggles
    2. Ordinary safety spectacles
    3. A peaked cap pulled down
    4. Dark tinted sunglasses
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Chemical splash goggles

    Liquids can run round the sides of spectacles. Goggles seal against the face, so splashes cannot reach the eyes. Check the safety data sheet for the eye protection the product needs.

    Source: PPE at Work Regulations 1992 and BS EN 166

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    Where should you look to find which type of chemical-resistant gloves a product needs?

    1. The product's safety data sheet
    2. The colour of the product tin
    3. The glove box's price label
    4. Any glove that feels thick enough
    Show the answer

    Answer A: The product's safety data sheet

    No glove resists every chemical; some solvents pass straight through certain materials. The safety data sheet names the right glove material, and the COSHH assessment sets it, so check before you start.

    Source: COSHH 2002 and safety data sheets

Revision notes

PPE is the last line of defence

Personal protective equipment only protects the person wearing it, and only when it is the right type, fits and is worn properly. That is why the law treats it as the last resort. Before PPE is chosen, the employer should remove the hazard or control it in other ways, such as using a different method, fitting guards or extraction, or putting up guardrails that protect everyone. PPE then deals with the risk that is left.

Who provides it and who looks after it

Your employer must assess what PPE the job needs, supply it free of charge, show you how to use it and keep it in good order. Since April 2022 this duty also covers most casual or "limb (b)" workers, not just employees. Your part is to wear it as you were trained, keep it clean, store it properly when you are not using it, and report straight away if it is lost, damaged or does not fit.

Head and eye protection

Check your safety helmet before each use: look for cracks, dents, a damaged harness or a worn chin strap. Do not paint it, drill it, or use solvents or stickers unless the maker allows it, as these can weaken the shell. Replace it after a heavy blow, even if no damage shows. Eye protection comes as spectacles, goggles or face shields, each suited to different hazards. Ordinary prescription glasses are not eye protection.

Hands and feet

Choose gloves for the actual hazard: cut-resistant for sharp materials, chemical-resistant gloves of the type named on the safety data sheet, or insulated gloves for heat. Gloves can be dangerous near rotating parts, such as a drill chuck, because they can be caught and pull your hand in. Safety footwear should have a protective toe cap and, on most sites, a midsole that stops nails coming through. Replace boots with split uppers or worn soles.

Hi-vis, hearing and breathing protection

High-visibility clothing helps drivers and plant operators see you. It only works if it is clean, fastened and in one piece, and site rules may set the type you must wear. Hearing protection and respiratory protective equipment (RPE) must match the hazard and fit the wearer. A dust mask will not protect against gases or vapours unless it is designed to, and tight-fitting masks only seal on a clean-shaven face.

Fit and compatibility

PPE that does not fit does not protect. When you wear more than one item at once, they must work together: for example, safety glasses that push a mask off your face, or earmuffs that stop a helmet sitting properly, reduce your protection. If something is uncomfortable or gets in the way of the job, tell your supervisor rather than leaving it off. Other sizes, styles or helmet-mounted versions are often available.
