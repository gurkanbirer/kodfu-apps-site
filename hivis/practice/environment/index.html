Environment: free HS&E test practice questions

Waste, spills and pollution, nuisance noise and dust, wildlife and saving energy and water.

6 original questions from the HiVis app for the CITB HS&E Operatives test, each with the answer and an explanation. No sign-up, nothing saved. These are not the real test questions: no app or website can show you those.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    What is the first step in the waste hierarchy?

    1. Prevent or reduce waste
    2. Recycle as much as possible
    3. Send waste to landfill
    4. Burn waste to recover energy
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Prevent or reduce waste

    Preventing waste comes first, for example by ordering the right quantities. Reuse, recycling and recovery follow, and disposal is the last option.

    Source: Waste (England and Wales) Regulations 2011

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    A man in an unmarked van stops at the site gate and offers to take away a pile of mixed waste for cash. He says no paperwork is needed.

    What should you do?

    1. Agree, as it saves the cost of a skip
    2. Agree, but take a photo of his number plate
    3. Refuse and tell your supervisor
    4. Help him load it quickly before anyone notices
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Refuse and tell your supervisor

    Waste must only go to a registered carrier with a transfer note. Unregistered collectors often fly-tip, and the site can be prosecuted for it.

    Source: Environmental Protection Act 1990 s34

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    What should be placed under a generator or pump that is running on site?

    1. A sheet of plywood
    2. A drip tray or plant nappy
    3. Nothing, if it is on gravel
    4. A tarpaulin to keep it dry
    Show the answer

    Answer B: A drip tray or plant nappy

    Drip trays and plant nappies catch fuel and oil leaks before they reach the ground or drains. Empty rainwater from them regularly.

    Source: Environment Agency pollution prevention guidance

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    While clearing scrub and small bushes from the edge of the site, you find a bird's nest with eggs in it.

    What should you do?

    1. Move the nest to a nearby hedge and carry on
    2. Stop work in that area and report it
    3. Carry on, as the birds can nest elsewhere
    4. Remove the eggs and keep the nest for the birds
    Show the answer

    Answer B: Stop work in that area and report it

    Active nests of wild birds are protected by law. Stop work nearby and report it so it can be dealt with properly.

    Source: Wildlife and Countryside Act 1981 s1

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    Who can be prosecuted if a construction site pollutes a river?

    1. Only the client who owns the land
    2. Only the principal contractor
    3. The company and any individuals responsible
    4. Only the person who owns the machine that leaked
    Show the answer

    Answer C: The company and any individuals responsible

    Environmental offences can be committed by companies and by individuals, including workers and managers whose actions or neglect caused the pollution.

    Source: Environmental Permitting Regulations 2016 reg 38

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    You are pumping rainwater out of an excavation. The water is brown with silt and the nearest outlet is a surface water drain.

    What should you do?

    1. Ask how it should be settled or treated first
    2. Pump it into the drain, as it is only rainwater
    3. Pump it onto the road so it drains away
    4. Pump it into the nearby stream instead
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Ask how it should be settled or treated first

    Silt smothers fish and plants, and surface water drains lead straight to rivers. Silty water must be settled, filtered or treated, and discharged only with the right permission.

    Source: Environment Agency pollution prevention guidance

Revision notes

Why it matters

Construction produces a large share of the UK's waste and can pollute rivers, land and air. Environmental laws, such as the Environmental Protection Act 1990, the rules on environmental permits and wildlife protection, apply to everyone on site. Companies and individuals can be prosecuted for polluting, mishandling waste or harming protected species. Your site induction should explain the environmental rules and who to tell when something goes wrong.

Waste hierarchy and segregation

The waste hierarchy puts the options in order: prevent waste first, then reuse, recycle, recover energy, and only dispose of what is left. Order the right amount of materials, store them so they are not damaged and reuse offcuts. Put waste into the right skip, such as timber, metal, plasterboard, general and hazardous waste. Keeping waste separate means more can be recycled and costs less. Plasterboard must be kept apart from general waste.

Duty of care for waste

Anyone who produces or handles waste has a legal duty of care to keep it safe and pass it only to someone authorised to take it, such as a registered waste carrier. Each load needs a waste transfer note, kept for at least 2 years. Hazardous waste, such as oily rags, solvents and asbestos, needs a consignment note, kept for at least 3 years in England. Burning waste on site and fly-tipping are illegal.

Drains and water

Surface water drains usually carry rainwater straight to a river or stream with no treatment. Foul drains go to a sewage treatment works. Never pour oil, fuel, paint, cement washings, chemicals or silty water into any drain or watercourse. Use the designated concrete washout area. Water pumped out of excavations often carries silt, which must be settled or treated before it is discharged with permission.

Fuel and oil

Store fuel and oil in secure, bunded tanks or containers. In England the bund must hold at least 110% of the largest container. Refuel in a designated area, away from drains and watercourses, with a spill kit at hand and a drip tray under the nozzle. Put drip trays or plant nappies under stationary plant such as generators and pumps, and empty rainwater from them so they do not overflow.

Dealing with spills

If a spill happens, stop the source if it is safe to do so, contain it with the spill kit, cover or block nearby drains, and tell your supervisor straight away. Do not wash a spill away with water. Used absorbents become hazardous waste. Serious pollution must be reported to the regulator: in England, the Environment Agency incident hotline is 0800 80 70 60. Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own regulators.

Nuisance to neighbours

Dust, noise, mud and light from sites can harm neighbours and lead to complaints and enforcement. Damp down dusty areas and haul roads, cover dusty loads and use wheel washes so mud is not carried onto public roads. Keep noisy work within the agreed working hours, switch off engines when not in use and keep equipment silencers fitted. Point site lighting away from homes and roads.

Wildlife, trees and finds

Nesting birds, all bats and their roosts, badgers and great crested newts are protected by law. If you find a nest, bats or other protected species, stop work in that area and report it. Do not cut, dig or move Japanese knotweed or soil containing it, as tiny pieces spread it. Keep out of fenced tree root protection areas. If you uncover old bones, pottery or other archaeological remains, stop and report.

Saving energy, water and materials

Switch off engines, lights, heaters and tools when they are not in use rather than leaving them idling. Turn off taps and hoses and report leaks. Look after materials so they are not damaged by weather or traffic and become waste. Small habits by everyone on site add up to big savings in cost, carbon and natural resources.
