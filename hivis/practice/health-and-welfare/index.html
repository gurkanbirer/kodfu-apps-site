Health and welfare: free HS&E test practice questions

Welfare facilities, staying fit for work, drugs and alcohol, skin, sun and infections such as Weil's disease.

6 original questions from the HiVis app for the CITB HS&E Operatives test, each with the answer and an explanation. No sign-up, nothing saved. These are not the real test questions: no app or website can show you those.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    When must welfare facilities be available on a construction site?

    1. Only once the building is weathertight
    2. Only after the first month of work
    3. From the start of the work until the end
    4. Only when more than 20 people are on site
    Show the answer

    Answer C: From the start of the work until the end

    People need toilets, washing, drinking water and somewhere to rest from the first day of work. CDM requires the principal contractor to provide welfare facilities throughout the construction phase, not just once the site is established.

    Source: CDM 2015 reg 13(4)(c) and Sch 2

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    What is dermatitis?

    1. An infection caught from dirty water on a building site
    2. A lung disease caused by breathing in dust
    3. Skin inflammation that causes redness and cracking
    4. A hearing problem caused by loud noise
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Skin inflammation that causes redness and cracking

    Dermatitis is skin inflammation. On site it is often caused by wet cement, solvents, resins and frequent wet work. It can become long-term and end a career, so prevention and early reporting matter.

    Source: HSE guidance on work-related contact dermatitis

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    You are about to work in a wet drainage ditch where rats have been seen. You have a fresh cut on the back of your hand.

    What should you do?

    1. Cover the cut with a waterproof dressing and wear gloves
    2. Leave the cut uncovered so it can heal in the air
    3. Put an ordinary fabric plaster on it and carry on
    4. Wash the cut in the ditch water before you start work
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Cover the cut with a waterproof dressing and wear gloves

    A waterproof dressing and gloves stop contaminated water reaching the cut. Wash your hands before eating, drinking or smoking.

    Source: HSE guidance on leptospirosis

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    Why can tiredness be dangerous on site?

    1. It makes you rush and work too quickly
    2. It makes your PPE feel uncomfortable
    3. It slows your reactions and judgement
    4. It makes you feel hungrier than usual
    Show the answer

    Answer C: It slows your reactions and judgement

    Fatigue slows reaction times and impairs judgement, making mistakes and accidents more likely, especially with plant or at height.

    Source: HSE guidance on fatigue and shift work (HSG256)

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    Which of these must be available in site welfare facilities?

    1. A supply of clean drinking water
    2. A vending machine for hot drinks
    3. A television in the rest area
    4. Free hot meals at lunchtime
    Show the answer

    Answer A: A supply of clean drinking water

    Drinking water is a basic need, especially in hot weather or heavy work, so CDM requires an adequate supply of wholesome drinking water on site. Drinks machines, televisions and meals are not legal requirements.

    Source: CDM 2015 Sch 2 para 3

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    After weeks of working with wet cement, the skin on your hands is red, itchy and starting to crack.

    What should you do?

    1. Report it and get your skin checked early
    2. Clean your hands with white spirit instead
    3. Ignore it, as site work always roughens skin
    4. Cover the cracks with plasters and carry on
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Report it and get your skin checked early

    These are early signs of dermatitis. Caught early it can clear up, but if exposure continues it can become long-term and stop you working with cement. Your employer can review gloves and washing and arrange skin checks.

    Source: HSE: Cement and dermatitis

Revision notes

Welfare facilities

Every construction site must have welfare facilities from the start of the work to the end. They include toilets; washing facilities with hot and cold, or warm, running water, soap and towels or dryers; showers where the work needs them; drinking water; somewhere to change and to store and dry clothing; and a rest area with tables, seating, a way to boil water and somewhere to eat. Facilities must be kept clean, lit and in working order.

Hygiene

Many substances on site, such as cement, lead and oils, can be swallowed or absorbed if they stay on your hands. Wash your hands and face before eating, drinking, smoking or using the toilet, and at the end of the shift. Eat in the rest area, not where you work. Keep work clothes away from your everyday clothes if they are contaminated, and never take hazardous materials home on your clothing.

Skin and sun

Dermatitis is inflammation of the skin that causes redness, itching, cracking and blistering. On site it is commonly caused by wet cement, solvents, resins and frequent wet work. Avoid skin contact, wear suitable gloves, wash off contamination quickly, dry your skin well and use after-work cream. Too much sun causes skin cancer: keep your top on, wear a hat with neck protection, use high-factor sunscreen and take breaks in the shade.

Weil's disease

Weil's disease, a serious form of leptospirosis, is caught from water or wet ground contaminated with rat urine, such as in drains, sewers, ditches and riverbanks. The bacteria enter through cuts, or through the eyes, nose or mouth. It starts like flu and can become very serious. Cover cuts with waterproof dressings, wear gloves, wash your hands before eating, and tell your doctor about your work if you fall ill.

Fitness for work

Alcohol and drugs, including some prescription and over-the-counter medicines, slow your reactions and affect your judgement, which is dangerous around plant and at height. Many sites test for drugs and alcohol. Tell your supervisor if a medicine might make you drowsy. Tiredness from long hours, shift work or poor sleep also causes mistakes. In hot weather, drink water regularly and take breaks in the shade.

Mental health

Construction workers face pressures such as long hours, time away from home and job insecurity, and the industry has a high suicide rate. If you are struggling, talk to someone you trust, your supervisor, a mental health first aider, your GP or a free helpline such as Samaritans on 116 123. Look out for workmates who seem withdrawn or not themselves, ask how they are and listen without judging.

Occupational health

Some work, such as using vibrating tools, working in noise, or with dusts, solvents and some chemicals, can slowly damage your health. Where the risk calls for it, your employer must arrange health surveillance, such as hearing tests, lung function tests or skin checks. These are to spot early signs of harm so action can be taken before it gets worse. Report symptoms early, such as tingling fingers, a persistent cough or sore skin.
