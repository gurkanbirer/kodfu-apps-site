Tools and equipment: free HS&E test practice questions

Using hand and power tools safely: guards, checks, training, abrasive wheels and cartridge tools.

6 original questions from the HiVis app for the CITB HS&E Operatives test, each with the answer and an explanation. No sign-up, nothing saved. These are not the real test questions: no app or website can show you those.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    You have been asked to use a type of power tool you have never used before. What should happen first?

    1. Read the instructions while you start the job
    2. Watch a workmate use it once, then carry on
    3. Get trained and authorised to use it
    4. Use it slowly until you get the hang of it
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Get trained and authorised to use it

    Under PUWER, people must have adequate training before using work equipment. Ask your supervisor to arrange it before you start.

    Source: PUWER 1998 reg 9

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    What should you do before changing the blade on a circular saw?

    1. Let the blade cool for a few seconds while it runs
    2. Switch it off at the trigger and leave it plugged in
    3. Ask someone to hold the guard back for you
    4. Unplug it from the power or take out the battery
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Unplug it from the power or take out the battery

    Disconnecting the power stops the saw starting by accident while your hands are near the blade.

    Source: PUWER 1998 and manufacturers' instructions

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    Why should you not grind with the side of a thin cutting disc?

    1. It leaves a rough and uneven finish on the work piece
    2. It wears out the bearings inside the machine
    3. It blunts the cutting edge of the disc quickly
    4. The disc is not built for side loads and can shatter
    Show the answer

    Answer D: The disc is not built for side loads and can shatter

    Thin cutting discs are designed to cut with their edge. Side pressure can break them apart at high speed.

    Source: HSE HSG17

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    Which nail gun trigger setting is least likely to fire a nail by accident?

    1. Contact (bump) trigger
    2. Sequential (single-shot) trigger
    3. Rapid-fire (multi-shot) trigger
    4. Continuous (hold-down) trigger
    Show the answer

    Answer B: Sequential (single-shot) trigger

    A sequential trigger only fires when the nose is pressed against the work first and then the trigger is pulled, so accidental firing is much less likely.

    Source: Manufacturers' guidance and HSE on nail guns

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    What is a whip check on a compressed air hose for?

    1. Stopping a hose whipping if a joint fails
    2. Measuring the air pressure in the hose
    3. Keeping dirt out of the air coupling
    4. Marking who the air hose belongs to
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Stopping a hose whipping if a joint fails

    If a coupling separates under pressure, the loose hose can whip violently and cause serious injuries. A whip check is a restraint that ties the two sides of the joint together.

    Source: HSE HSG39 Compressed air safety

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    Why should you never carry or lower a power tool by its cable?

    1. It can damage the cable and its connections
    2. It makes the tool's motor wear out sooner
    3. It uses more power when switched on later
    4. It makes the cable too short to reach
    Show the answer

    Answer A: It can damage the cable and its connections

    The cable and plug are not made to take the tool's weight. Pulling on them can loosen connections or split the insulation, exposing live wires. Use a rope or tool bag to raise or lower tools.

    Source: HSE HSG107 Maintaining portable electrical equipment

Revision notes

PUWER in a nutshell

The Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regulations 1998 (PUWER) cover every tool and machine used at work. Employers must provide equipment that is suitable for the job, keep it maintained and inspected where needed, fit guards and controls, and make sure users have the training, instructions and authority to use it. Workers must use equipment only as trained, never remove guards or safety devices, and report faults straight away.

Hand tools

Most hand tool injuries come from using the wrong tool or a damaged one. Look out for loose hammer heads, split handles, blunt blades and chisels with a "mushroomed" head, which can throw off sharp fragments when struck. Do not use a screwdriver as a chisel or lever. Keep cutting edges sharp and guarded, and carry tools in a holster or bag rather than in your pockets.

Power tools and guards

Check the tool and guard before use and never remove, tie back or wedge a guard. Disconnect the power or take the battery out before changing blades, bits or discs. Clamp the work piece rather than holding it by hand, use the right blade for the material, and let the tool stop before you put it down. Never carry or lower a tool by its cable.

Abrasive wheels

Abrasive wheels and cutting discs can burst at high speed. Only a trained, competent person should fit or change them. Before fitting, check the wheel is undamaged, is the right type for the job and that the maximum speed marked on it is not less than the speed of the machine. Use the correct flanges, keep the guard adjusted and do not grind with the side of a thin cutting disc.

Cartridge tools and nail guns

Cartridge-operated fixing tools fire a nail or stud using an explosive charge. Only trained, authorised people may use them. Choose the cartridge strength by following the maker's guidance, starting with the lowest that will do the job, and never fire into brittle, thin or unsuitable material. If a cartridge misfires, follow the maker's misfire procedure. On nail guns, a sequential (single-shot) trigger is much less likely to fire by accident than a contact trigger.

Petrol cutters and air tools

Petrol disc cutters can kick back violently if the upper front part of the blade catches. Hold them firmly with both hands, stand to the side of the cutting line and keep the guard set correctly. Refuel only when the engine is cold and away from ignition sources. With compressed air tools, check hoses and couplings, fit whip checks, turn the air off before disconnecting, and never point air at yourself or others.
