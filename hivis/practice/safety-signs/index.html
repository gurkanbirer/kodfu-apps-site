Safety signs: free HS&E test practice questions

Sign shapes and colours, what each type means and common site signals.

6 original questions from the HiVis app for the CITB HS&E Operatives test, each with the answer and an explanation. No sign-up, nothing saved. These are not the real test questions: no app or website can show you those.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    A round sign with a red border and a red diagonal bar across a black picture means what?

    1. You must not do the thing shown
    2. You must do the thing shown
    3. There is a hazard ahead
    4. The equipment shown is nearby
    Show the answer

    Answer A: You must not do the thing shown

    Red circles with a diagonal bar are prohibition signs. They ban the activity shown, such as smoking or entry by pedestrians.

    Source: Safety Signs and Signals Regulations 1996 Sch 1

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    A green rectangular sign shows a white running figure and an arrow. What does it show?

    1. The route vehicles must take
    2. An area where running is allowed
    3. A place where you must hurry
    4. The way to an emergency exit
    Show the answer

    Answer D: The way to an emergency exit

    Green safe condition signs show the way to safety. The running figure and arrow mark an escape route or emergency exit.

    Source: Safety Signs and Signals Regulations 1996 Sch 1

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    Which type of sign would you expect to see saying "No pedestrian access"?

    1. A warning sign: a yellow triangle
    2. A mandatory sign: a blue circle
    3. A prohibition sign: a red circle and bar
    4. A safe condition sign: a green rectangle
    Show the answer

    Answer C: A prohibition sign: a red circle and bar

    Banning pedestrians from an area is a prohibition, so it uses the red circle and diagonal bar, often with supporting text.

    Source: Safety Signs and Signals Regulations 1996 Sch 1

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    What is a supplementary sign?

    1. A spare sign kept in case the main one is damaged
    2. A sign that only applies at night
    3. A sign giving extra information with a main sign
    4. A temporary sign used only by visitors
    Show the answer

    Answer C: A sign giving extra information with a main sign

    A supplementary sign adds information, usually words, to a main safety sign, for example "Hard hat area" under a mandatory symbol.

    Source: Safety Signs and Signals Regulations 1996 Sch 1 Part I

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    A blue circular sign shows a white figure wearing a safety harness. What does it mean?

    1. Harnesses are available nearby
    2. Harnesses may be worn if you wish
    3. You must wear a safety harness in this area
    4. Harnesses must not be worn in this area
    Show the answer

    Answer C: You must wear a safety harness in this area

    A blue circle is a mandatory sign, so the instruction shown must be followed. A harness sign is used where there is a fall risk that other measures do not fully control.

    Source: Safety Signs and Signals Regulations 1996 Sch 1

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    A yellow warning triangle shows a black exclamation mark. What does it mean?

    1. General danger: take care
    2. You must stop here
    3. First aid is nearby
    4. The exit is this way
    Show the answer

    Answer A: General danger: take care

    The exclamation mark is the general warning symbol, used for a hazard that has no picture of its own. It usually comes with a supplementary sign saying what the danger is.

    Source: Safety Signs and Signals Regulations 1996 Sch 1

Revision notes

Prohibition signs

A prohibition sign tells you something you must not do. It is a white circle with a red border and a red diagonal bar, with a black picture underneath the bar. Common examples on site are no smoking, no naked flames, no unauthorised access, no pedestrians and do not drink this water. If you see one, the activity is banned in that area for everyone, whatever their trade or seniority.

Mandatory signs

A mandatory sign tells you something you must do. It is a solid blue circle with a white picture. Examples include wear a safety helmet, wear eye protection, wear hearing protection, wear a harness, use the footpath and keep this door shut. The instruction applies from the point where the sign is placed, so put the PPE on before you go past it, not once you are inside.

Warning signs

A warning sign tells you about a hazard. It is a yellow triangle with a black border and a black picture. Examples include danger of electric shock (a lightning flash), overhead loads, forklift trucks, slippery surface, deep excavation and asbestos. A warning sign does not tell you what to do: it tells you to be alert and to follow the controls set out for that hazard.

Safe condition and fire equipment signs

Safe condition signs show the way to safety or help. They are green rectangles or squares with a white picture: emergency exits and escape routes (a running figure and arrow), first aid, eyewash stations, emergency telephones and assembly points. Fire equipment signs are red rectangles or squares with a white picture. They show where to find extinguishers, fire alarm call points, fire hoses and fire blankets.

Supplementary signs and signals

A supplementary sign adds words to a main sign, such as "Hard hat area" under a mandatory symbol. Signs must be kept clean, visible and in the right place, and taken down or covered when the hazard has gone, so people keep trusting them. Not every signal is a sign: alarms, flashing beacons, spoken instructions and hand signals are also used to warn and direct people on site.

Colours, stripes and alarm signals

Safety colours always mean the same thing. Red is for prohibition, danger alarms and fire-fighting equipment; yellow or amber for warning; blue for mandatory action; green for emergency escape, first aid and no danger. Obstacles and places where people could fall, collide or be hit by falling objects can be marked with yellow and black, or red and white, stripes at about 45 degrees. Acoustic signals must be clearly louder than the background noise, and the signal for evacuation must be continuous.
