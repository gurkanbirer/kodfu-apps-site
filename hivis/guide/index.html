CITB Health, Safety and Environment test: a plain-English guide

The CITB Health, Safety and Environment (HS&E) test is the test most people need to pass before they can apply for a CSCS card to work on UK building sites. This guide explains which version you need, what the test looks like, how to book it, what to bring on the day and how to get ready.

Who needs the HS&E test?

Most CSCS cards need two things: a construction qualification (finished or in progress) and a pass in the right CITB HS&E test, or an alternative that CSCS accepts. CSCS says the pass must be from the last two years when you apply for your card.

The test is owned and run by CITB (the Construction Industry Training Board). CSCS does not run the test and you cannot book it through CSCS. The CSCS Card Finder tells you which card fits your job and which test level that card needs. HiVis has its own Card Finder as a starting point, but card rules change, so confirm with CSCS before you book.

The three types of test

CITB offers three levels. Which one you take depends on the card you are applying for.

TestWhat it coversUsually needed for
OperativesBasic health, safety and environmental awareness for working on siteLabourer, Trainee and Apprentice cards, most Blue Skilled Worker cards and most Gold Advanced Craft cards
SpecialistThe Operatives content plus questions on one specialist areaThe Gold Supervisor card, and Blue Skilled Worker and Gold Advanced Craft cards in some occupations
Managers and Professionals (MAP)The Operatives content plus law, management, CDM and higher-risk activitiesThe Black Manager card and the Academically or Professionally Qualified Person cards

Source: CSCS, Health and safety test.

Specialist tests

CITB lists these specialist areas. You take the one that matches your work:

  • Supervisory
  • Demolition
  • Plumbing (JIB)
  • Highway works
  • Specialist work at height
  • Lifts and escalators
  • Tunnelling
  • HVACR: heating and plumbing services; pipefitting and welding; ductwork; and further HVACR options such as refrigeration and air conditioning

The list can change. Check CITB's specialist test page for the current options before you book. HiVis covers seven tests: Operatives, Managers and Professionals, and the Supervisory, Work at height, Demolition, Plumbing and Highway works specialist tests. It does not cover the Lifts and escalators, Tunnelling or HVACR tests.

Format and pass mark

Every HS&E test
Questions50
Time45 minutes
Pass mark45 out of 50 (90%)
WhereOn a computer at a test centre
Price£23.50 per test in CITB's booking terms from April 2026 (check when you book)

CITB's pages for the Operatives, Specialist and Managers and Professionals tests give the same 50 questions, 45 minutes and 45 to pass. The MAP pass mark used to be 46 out of 50, so older guides may still show that figure. Some older CITB and CSCS pages also still show the earlier £22.50 price. CITB says it can change prices, so the price on the booking screen is the one that counts.

The on-screen text is always in English. CITB offers an English or Welsh voice-over for every test, and the Operatives test can be taken with a voice-over in other languages, such as Polish or Romanian, which you choose when you book. Check CITB's support for the HS&E test page for the current languages and for other help you can ask for, such as reasonable adjustments.

What the questions are like

Since June 2019 the test has been a single section. The separate behavioural case studies that older guides describe were removed, and questions about how you would behave on site are now built into the 50 questions. If a guide or app still talks about "38 questions plus 12 case-study questions", it describes the old format.

  • Most questions have one right answer from a list.
  • Some ask you to choose two or three answers. You need all of them right to get the mark.
  • Some questions use a picture, for example a safety sign.
  • The MAP test also has drag-and-drop questions where you put steps in the right order.
  • Each candidate gets a set mix of easier and harder questions.

Expect questions about real situations: what you should do when you find a hazard, who you report something to, or which option is the safest way to do a job. Learning the rules is only half of it. The other half is choosing the safe action, even when a quicker way looks tempting.

How to book

You book with CITB, not CSCS. CITB's tests are delivered by Pearson VUE, its test provider.

  • Online: through the booking link on the CITB website.
  • By phone: 0344 994 4488 (Welsh language line 0344 994 4490; special assistance 0344 994 4491), as listed on Pearson VUE's CITB page.

When you book, choose the right test level (and specialist area if you need one), your voice-over language and a test centre. Make sure the name on your booking is exactly the name on the ID you will bring. Be careful with sites that resell test bookings at a higher price: the official route is through CITB.

What to bring on the day

CITB's HS&E Test ID Policy is strict. In short:

  • Bring one primary ID that shows your full name. Accepted primary ID includes a passport, a UK or European photocard driving licence, or an eVisa shown through the UKVI View and Prove service. The full list is in the policy.
  • Documents must be originals, not photocopies or photos on your phone. An eVisa is the exception.
  • If you have no primary ID, you can bring two forms of secondary ID, one from each of CITB's two lists.
  • CITB has an ID waiver form for exceptional cases only. Without acceptable ID you will not be allowed to sit the test.
  • Bring your booking confirmation and arrive at least 15 minutes before your test starts.

Read the current ID policy a few days before your test, not on the morning itself.

Results, resits and validity

Every candidate gets written confirmation of whether they passed or failed. If you fail, you can book another test, and you pay again for the new booking.

When you apply for a CSCS card, your pass must be from the last two years. If your pass is older than that when you apply or renew, you will usually need to take the test again. Check the rules for your card with CSCS. For reference, CSCS lists the card fee as £36 (the Apprentice card is free), rising to £42 for applications paid on or after 01/02/2027.

Accepted alternatives to the HS&E test

CSCS accepts a few alternatives in certain cases. Each must have been passed or completed within two years of your card application:

  • Recognised apprenticeship (Apprentice cards only): the health and safety requirements set by your Apprenticeship Managing Agency, with the evidence CSCS asks for.
  • CIC Health and Safety Test: accepted in place of the MAP test, but only for Academically Qualified Person and Professionally Qualified Person cards, and for the five-year Trainee card if you are registered on a construction-related degree that leads to an AQP card.
  • SOLAS Safe Pass: accepted in place of the Operatives or Specialist test, but not the Supervisory or MAP test.

Details and the evidence you need are on the CSCS page HS&E test exemptions.

How to prepare

  1. Check which test you need. Use the CSCS Card Finder first. Taking the wrong level wastes the fee.
  2. Learn topic by topic. The Operatives core covers 19 areas, from legal duties and PPE to excavations and the environment. Specialist and MAP tests add their own topics on top.
  3. Read the explanations, not just the answers. Many questions are about judgement, so understanding why the safe option is right helps more than memorising.
  4. Practise against the clock. 50 questions in 45 minutes is under a minute each. Sit full mocks and aim to score well above 45 before test day.
  5. Learn the English site words. Even with a voice-over, the screen text is in English. Reading the questions aloud while you practise helps.
  6. Use official material if you want it. CITB sells official revision books, apps and downloads.
