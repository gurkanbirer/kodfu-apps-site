Train strikes: refunds, Delay Repay and season ticket days

On a strike day your rights are the same as for any other cancellation or delay: a full refund if you don’t travel, Delay Repay if you travel and arrive late. Season ticket holders may also get a day back, but that is set by each train company, not by a national rule.

Checked 28/11/2026 against the National Rail Conditions of Travel (edition from 30/07/2026), National Rail and train company pages. National Rail’s industrial action page says no further nationwide industrial action has been announced and keeps its rules for reference.

Single, return and Advance tickets

  • You don’t travel: if your train is cancelled, delayed or rescheduled, you get a full refund with no admin fee from the retailer you bought from, Advance tickets included (Conditions of Travel 30.1). See cancelled trains.
  • You travel and arrive late: claim Delay Repay as usual. The delay is measured against the timetable in force that day, so if the company published a reduced strike timetable in advance, that timetable is the baseline.
  • Another day: during past national strikes, the industry let people use tickets on set other days. Those were one-off arrangements; check the company’s strike page at the time.

Season tickets on strike days

The Conditions of Travel leave season compensation to each company’s Passenger’s Charter (33.3). National Rail’s archived industrial action page said that holders of monthly or longer seasons who could not travel because a strike cancelled their service, and Flexi holders with an activated day, could claim 100% for those days through Delay Repay; weekly season holders who bought after a strike was announced were covered only if they travelled and were delayed. What train companies say today:

  • Southeastern: Flexi, monthly or longer season holders can claim a full day back for each strike day they chose not to travel, with one claim per day and the reason “Industrial Action – did not travel”, from the strike day up to 28 days after. Weekly seasons only if the service was cancelled or delayed.
  • Transport for Wales: season tickets bought from TfW can claim for days they could not travel because of industrial action, choosing “Strike Action” as the reason.
  • Merseyrail: monthly and annual season and Railpass holders who could not travel get a day’s travel back, if they claim within 28 days of the strike.
  • Greater Anglia: its claim site asks for one claim per strike date to claim a full day’s refund.
  • Other companies: check the company’s strike or Delay Repay page on the day; links are in our train company guides.

Example. A monthly season of £400 is worth £10.00 a journey, so a full day (a return) is about £20.00 for each strike day you could not travel, where your company pays a full day.

Taxis and hotels

National Rail has said it cannot refund taxi or hotel bills on strike days, but your ticket may be accepted on another train company or by another route. If you are stranded, companies will arrange alternative transport or accommodation where they reasonably can (Conditions of Travel 28.2). Ask staff first.

Complaints about strikes

The Rail Ombudsman does not look at complaints about strike action itself. A complaint about how a refund or claim was handled is a different matter: see challenging a rejected claim.

Strike questions

Can I get a refund if my train is cancelled because of a strike?

Yes. If you decide not to travel, you get a full refund with no admin fee from the retailer you bought from, Advance tickets included.

Do season ticket holders get compensation for strike days?

Often, but it depends on the train company. Several, including Southeastern, Transport for Wales and Merseyrail, let monthly or longer season holders claim a day back for each strike day they could not travel.

Is the delay on a strike day measured against the normal timetable?

Against the timetable in force that day. If a reduced timetable was published in advance, the delay is measured against it.
