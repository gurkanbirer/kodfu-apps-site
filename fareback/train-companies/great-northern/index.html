Great Northern Delay Repay: how to claim for a late train

Great Northern pays Delay Repay when your train arrives at least 15 minutes late (DR15). Claim within 28 days of your delayed journey, online or by post. Here is how much you get, how to claim and what to do if Great Northern says no.

How much Great Northern pays

The delay is measured at the station where your journey ends, against the timetable in force that day. The percentages are of the price you paid.

Arrival delaySingle ticketReturn ticket (one way delayed)
15 to 29 minutes25%12.5%
30 to 59 minutes50%25%
60 to 119 minutes100%50%
120 minutes or more100%100%

Example. You paid £24.60 for a single and your Great Northern train reached your destination 34 minutes late: 50% of the fare, £12.30. With a £48.00 return and only the way home 34 minutes late: 25% of the return fare, £12.00. An hour late on a single: the whole £24.60 back.

Does Great Northern pay automatically?

Auto Delay Repay. For Key Smartcard tickets (keyGo included) when you have signed up for Auto Delay Repay and touched in and out, on Thameslink, Southern, Great Northern or Gatwick Express. You get an email within 48 hours and confirm the claim; you have 28 days from the delayed journey to do so. A claim you make by hand cancels the automatic one, so check your email first. It only works when the whole journey is on one Key ticket.

Anything it does not cover, you claim yourself.

How to claim from Great Northern, step by step

  1. Note the train you planned to catch, its scheduled arrival and the time you really arrived. Claims are for the train you intended to use, even if you caught a later one.
  2. Check the delay against the table above, at the station where your journey ended.
  3. Get your ticket evidence ready (list below).
  4. Open Great Northern’s claim form and enter the journey, the delay and your ticket.
  5. Choose how to be paid and submit. Keep the claim reference.
  6. Watch for the answer. If it is refused or less than you expected, challenge it (see below).

Postal claims: Yes: the GTR postal form, sent to Freepost RUHE-TXXU-GHHA, GTR, Derby DE1 9RQ.

What you need

  • Paper ticket: the ticket, its receipt, or a scan or photo of the season ticket and photocard
  • eTicket or barcode ticket: the ticket number (UTN) or an image
  • Key Smartcard: the card number
  • Oyster or contactless: a TfL journey history print-out
  • One image per claim, up to 5MB

How Great Northern pays: Online: bank transfer (BACS), e-Voucher (needs an online account), PayPal, cashable National Rail vouchers or a donation to Samaritans or Railway Children. Auto Delay Repay can also pay to a Visa card. Postal claims have fewer options.

Season tickets on Great Northern

Each journey on a season is valued at the season price divided by 464 (annual), 240 (six-monthly), 120 (quarterly), 40 (monthly) or 10 (weekly); a Flexi Season is divided by 16. A delay of 120 minutes or more pays 100% of a return journey, a full day's travel, and you can make only one 120-minute claim a day.

Example. An annual season costing £4,640 gives a journey value of £4,640 ÷ 464 = £10.00. Arriving 20 minutes late pays 25% of that, £2.50; 34 minutes late pays 50%, £5.00. Small amounts, but a commuter delayed twice a week has around 90 claims a year.

Why Great Northern claims get rejected

  • You claimed by hand for a journey that Auto Delay Repay had already raised: the manual claim cancels the automatic one.
  • You made a second 120-minute claim on the same day on a season ticket: one full day's travel is the cap.
  • A missed connection where too little time was left for the change, or without a ticket for the whole journey: GTR pays missed connections only when enough time was left and you hold a ticket that covers the entire journey.
  • You gave the train you actually caught instead of the one you planned to catch. The National Rail Conditions of Travel (33.1) ask for the train you intended to use.
  • The claim arrived after the deadline.
  • The delay at your destination was under the threshold. It is measured against the timetable in force that day, so a published engineering-works timetable is the baseline, not the normal one (Conditions of Travel 32.1).
  • The ticket image does not show what the company needs, such as the ticket number, journey and price.
  • The journey was already claimed, including by an automatic claim.

If you think the decision is wrong, ask Great Northern to review it with your evidence (Great Northern complaints and contact). Then follow our step-by-step challenge guide (with a letter you can copy) and, if needed, the Rail Ombudsman.

Good to know about Great Northern

  • Great Northern is run by the same company as Southern, Thameslink and Gatwick Express (GTR).
  • Delays caused by planned engineering work in the published timetable are not compensated.

Great Northern Delay Repay questions

Is Great Northern DR15 or DR30?

It is DR15: Great Northern pays from 15 minutes late. The amounts are in the table above. Checked on Great Northern’s own pages on 28/11/2026.

How long do I have to claim from Great Northern?

Claim within 28 days of your delayed journey, online or by post. Claiming within a few days is easiest: you still have the ticket and know which train you planned to catch.

Does Great Northern pay compensation automatically?

Auto Delay Repay: For Key Smartcard tickets (keyGo included) when you have signed up for Auto Delay Repay and touched in and out, on Thameslink, Southern, Great Northern or Gatwick Express. You get an email within 48 hours and confirm the claim; you have 28 days from the delayed journey to do so. A claim you make by hand cancels the automatic one, so check your email first. It only works when the whole journey is on one Key ticket.

I bought my ticket from another website. Who do I claim from?

From the train company whose train delayed you, not the website or app that sold the ticket. If your journey used several companies, you can send the claim to any of them and it must pass it on to the one responsible (National Rail Conditions of Travel 32.1). Refunds for journeys you did not make are different: those go to the retailer.

What if Great Northern rejects or underpays my claim?

Ask Great Northern to look again, with your evidence, then make a complaint. Train companies should usually give a full response within 20 working days. If you are still unhappy after its final response, or 40 working days have passed, take it to the Rail Ombudsman, which is free. See how to challenge a rejected claim.
