Season ticket Delay Repay: what each late train is worth

You can claim Delay Repay on a season ticket for every delayed journey. The train company works out what one journey is worth by dividing the season price by a set number of journeys, then pays the usual percentage of that. Each claim is small, which is exactly why commuters miss so much.

Checked 28/11/2026 against the National Rail Conditions of Travel (edition from 30/07/2026), National Rail and train company pages. Season compensation follows each company’s Passenger’s Charter (Conditions of Travel 33.3), so check your company’s guide.

The daily value maths

Most companies value one journey on a season like this:

Season ticketDivide the price byExample
Weekly10£60 ÷ 10 = £6.00
Monthly40£400 ÷ 40 = £10.00
Quarterly (some companies)120£1,200 ÷ 120 = £10.00
Six-monthly (some companies)240£2,400 ÷ 240 = £10.00
Annual464£4,640 ÷ 464 = £10.00
Flexi Season (8 days in 28)16£180 ÷ 16 = £11.25

Then the band applies to that journey value: on a DR15 company, 25% for 15 to 29 minutes, 50% for 30 to 59 minutes and 100% for 60 to 119 minutes. For 120 minutes or more, many companies pay a return journey’s value (twice the single value): for example GWR, South Western Railway, Chiltern, c2c and the GTR companies (Southern, Thameslink, Great Northern, Gatwick Express). Avanti West Coast and CrossCountry publish 100% of a journey for that band.

Some companies write the same maths differently. West Midlands Railway divides by return journeys (annual 232, monthly 20, weekly 5, Flexi 8) with half the percentages; East Midlands Railway and TransPennine Express publish one divisor per band; Hull Trains and Lumo use 928 for 30 to 59 minutes and 464 from 60 minutes. The results are the same. Merseyrail is different: it pays seasons on average punctuality when the ticket ends, not per journey.

Over a year: two 15 to 29-minute delays a week on that £4,640 annual season is about 90 claims of £2.50, roughly £225. Add a few longer delays and many commuters on the often-late lines are owed several hundred pounds a year. Most of it is never claimed because each claim is small and the form takes time.

The one-day cap

You cannot be paid more than your ticket is worth (Conditions of Travel 32.2), and for a season that means one day’s travel. Once a 120-minute delay has used up the day, companies refuse more claims for that day; the GTR companies allow only one 120-minute claim a day, and South Western Railway never pays more than two single journeys for one delay. Transport for Wales takes at most two claims a day.

How to claim on a season ticket

  1. Claim each delayed journey separately, within the deadline, usually 28 days.
  2. Give the train you planned to catch and when you arrived.
  3. Upload the season ticket and photocard, or give the smartcard number. Many companies let you save the season in a Delay Repay account so you don’t upload it every time.
  4. Check whether your company raises claims for you: some automatic schemes cover seasons on a smartcard bought direct, such as South Western Railway (Touch), GWR (seasons on a GWR smartcard) and the GTR companies (Key Smartcard). Paper seasons usually need a claim by hand.

Strike days

There is no national rule in the Conditions of Travel for season tickets on strike days; each company decides. National Rail’s industrial action page, now kept for reference only, said holders of monthly or longer seasons and activated Flexi days could claim 100% for strike days through Delay Repay. Companies that say so today include Southeastern (Flexi, monthly or longer: one claim per strike day, within 28 days), Transport for Wales (seasons bought from TfW, reason “Strike Action”) and Merseyrail (monthly and annual: a day’s travel, within 28 days). More on the strikes page.

Refunding an unused season

If you stop commuting, you can return a season for a partial refund (Conditions of Travel 40). The refund deducts the cost of a return journey for each day since the start, plus an admin charge of no more than £10. A weekly season needs at least 3 days left and a 1 to 10-month season at least 7 days; an annual season has no cash value after ten months and twelve days. A Flexi Season refund deducts an Anytime return for each day used.

Season ticket questions

Can I claim Delay Repay on a season ticket?

Yes, for each delayed journey. The company values one journey at the season price divided by a set number (for example 464 for an annual, 40 for a monthly, 10 for a weekly and 16 for a Flexi Season) and pays the usual percentage of it.

How much is a 15-minute delay worth on an annual season?

On a DR15 company, 25% of the journey value: the price divided by 464, divided by 4. On a £4,640 annual season that is £2.50.

Can I claim twice in one day?

Yes, for the morning and evening journeys, up to the value of one day’s travel. A delay of 120 minutes or more usually uses the whole day.

Do season ticket holders get compensation for strikes?

It depends on the train company: many let monthly, annual and Flexi holders claim a day back for each strike day they could not travel. See strikes.
