MOT and vehicle tax dates: what UK drivers need to know

Two dates matter for every vehicle you keep on the road: when its MOT runs out and when its vehicle tax is due. Miss either and you can be fined. This guide explains how both work, how to find your dates for free on GOV.UK, and how to make sure you never miss them.

When the first MOT is due

In Great Britain (England, Scotland and Wales), a vehicle needs its first MOT by the third anniversary of its registration. After that it needs one every year, by the anniversary of its last MOT. Some vehicles must be tested from one year old: GOV.UK's MOT fees table shows which. A few vehicles do not need an MOT at all; see vehicles exempt from MOT.

In Northern Ireland the rules are different and testing is run by the DVA. Cars and motorcycles have their first MOT at four years old, and light goods vehicles at three. See how the MOT scheme works on nidirect.

Getting an MOT early and keeping your date

You do not have to wait until the last day. In Great Britain you can have the MOT done up to a month minus a day before it runs out and still keep the same renewal date. For example, if your MOT runs out on 20/06, the earliest test that keeps the 20/06 date is on 21/05. If you test earlier than that, the new MOT runs for a year minus a day from the day it passes, so you lose some time.

Booking a few weeks early also gives you time to fix anything that fails before the old certificate runs out. GOV.UK's guide Getting an MOT covers booking, fees, retests and what happens after the test.

Driving without a valid MOT

GOV.UK says you can be fined up to £1,000 for driving a vehicle without a valid MOT. The only times you may drive a vehicle without one are to or from a place where it will be repaired, or to an MOT test that is already booked.

A valid MOT also matters for vehicle tax: GOV.UK says that when you tax at a Post Office you might need to show evidence of an MOT that is valid when the tax starts.

Check your MOT and tax dates yourself

You do not need to dig out paperwork. GOV.UK has two free services that only need the registration number:

  • Check MOT history shows when the vehicle's MOT runs out, plus its past test results and any advisories.
  • Check if a vehicle is taxed shows whether it is taxed or SORN and when the tax is next due.

Your MOT expiry date is also printed on your last MOT certificate. While you are on GOV.UK, the free vehicle recall check tells you whether there is an open safety recall for your make and model.

Vehicle tax basics

  • Every vehicle on the road must be taxed, even if it is in a class that pays £0. The only alternative is to declare it off the road with a SORN (see below).
  • To tax, you need one of these: a recent tax reminder or "last chance" letter from DVLA, the V5C log book in your name, or the green "new keeper" slip if you have just bought the vehicle.
  • Ways to pay: online, by phone or at a Post Office that does vehicle tax. You can pay in one go or by Direct Debit (online or at a Post Office, not by phone).
  • Tax does not move with the vehicle. When you buy a used vehicle, the seller's tax is not passed to you. You must tax it before you drive it, even if you are keeping it at the same address. The seller gets a refund for any full months left once DVLA is told about the sale. See tell DVLA you've sold, transferred or bought a vehicle.

Start on GOV.UK's Tax your vehicle page. The official online service is linked from that page.

SORN: taking a vehicle off the road

A SORN (Statutory Off Road Notification) tells DVLA that you have stopped using a vehicle on public roads, for example because it is kept in a garage or on a drive while it is being restored. Once it is SORN:

  • you do not need to tax it, and you get a refund for any full months of tax left;
  • you cannot use it on the road until you tax it again.

To make a SORN online you need the vehicle registered in your name and either the 11-digit reference number from the V5C or the 16-digit reference from a tax reminder. You do not need a SORN for a vehicle you have sold: tell DVLA about the sale instead. See Make a SORN.

The V5C log book

The V5C (vehicle log book, or registration certificate) shows who the registered keeper is. You need it to tax the vehicle, make a SORN and tell DVLA when you sell it. Keep it somewhere safe, not in the car, and keep its details up to date: if you move house or change something about the vehicle, update it through GOV.UK's change vehicle details service. If you have lost it, you can get a replacement (there may be a fee).

Reminders

DVLA sends vehicle tax reminders to the registered keeper, which is one more reason to keep your V5C address right. For the MOT, GOV.UK has a free MOT reminder service by text or email. If you look after several vehicles, or want insurance, service and other dates in the same place, an app can help too. Whatever you use, a reminder is only a helper: you stay responsible for having your vehicle taxed, MOT-tested and insured on time.
