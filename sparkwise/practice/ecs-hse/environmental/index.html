  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    Which law places a 'duty of care' on anyone who produces, carries or disposes of waste?

    1. Environmental Protection Act 1990
    2. Manual Handling Operations Regulations 1992
    3. Work at Height Regulations 2005
    4. Electricity at Work Regulations 1989
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Environmental Protection Act 1990

    Section 34 of the Environmental Protection Act 1990 sets out the waste duty of care.

    Source: Environmental Protection Act 1990 s34

  2. Question 2 · True or false?

    A business can be held responsible if waste it handed to someone else is later fly-tipped.

    1. True
    2. False
    Show the answer

    Answer A: True

    The duty of care means you must take reasonable steps to make sure your waste is handled lawfully, including checking who takes it.

    Source: Environmental Protection Act 1990 s34

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    When dealing with site waste, which option should be used only as a last resort?

    1. Disposal to landfill
    2. Reuse on another job
    3. Recycling the material
    4. Preventing the waste
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Disposal to landfill

    Disposal is the last resort, used only when nothing else is practicable.

    Source: Waste (England and Wales) Regulations 2011

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    Your site has separate skips for metal, timber, cardboard and general waste. What is the main benefit?

    1. More is recycled and hazardous waste kept out
    2. Each trade can then be charged for its own skip
    3. Mixed skips are illegal on all sites
    4. Separate skips are cheaper to hire
    Show the answer

    Answer A: More is recycled and hazardous waste kept out

    Segregation keeps recyclable materials clean and stops hazardous waste contaminating general waste.

    Source: Waste (England and Wales) Regulations 2011

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    How should hazardous waste be handled on site?

    1. Taken home and put in a household bin
    2. Mixed with general waste to dilute it
    3. Kept separate for an authorised carrier
    4. Put in the nearest skip with other waste
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Kept separate for an authorised carrier

    Hazardous waste needs separate storage and a consignment note when it is moved.

    Source: Hazardous Waste Regulations 2005

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    What does a crossed-out wheelie bin symbol on a product mean?

    1. It is made from recycled bins
    2. It can go in any skip on site
    3. It is safe to burn on site
    4. Do not put it in general waste
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Do not put it in general waste

    The symbol shows the product is covered by WEEE rules and must be collected separately.

    Source: WEEE Regulations 2013

  7. Question 7 · Choose one answer

    Diesel is leaking from a generator on site. What should you do?

    1. Hose it into the nearest surface water drain
    2. Stop it if safe, contain it and report it
    3. Cover it with soil and say nothing
    4. Leave it to evaporate in the sun
    Show the answer

    Answer B: Stop it if safe, contain it and report it

    Quick containment with the spill kit stops fuel reaching drains and water. Report it so it is cleaned up properly.

    Source: Environment Agency pollution prevention guidance

  8. Question 8 · Choose one answer

    Secondary containment (a bund) for fuel and oil stores should hold at least what share of the largest container?

    1. 100%
    2. 50%
    3. 75%
    4. 110%
    Show the answer

    Answer D: 110%

    A bund that holds at least 110% of the largest container can contain a total failure, with a margin to spare.

    Source: Oil Storage Regulations 2001

  9. Question 9 · Choose one answer

    How can a local authority control noisy construction work?

    1. By fining every worker on the site
    2. By banning all power tools nationally
    3. By setting permitted hours and methods
    4. By issuing parking tickets to workers
    Show the answer

    Answer C: By setting permitted hours and methods

    Councils can serve notices restricting hours and methods, and contractors can agree conditions in advance.

    Source: EPA 1990 s79 · Control of Pollution Act 1974

  10. Question 10 · Choose one answer

    Which of these is a simple way to save energy on site?

    1. Switch off lights, heaters and engines when idle
    2. Leave the generator running overnight
    3. Keep cabin heaters on all day with the windows open
    4. Leave tools on charge permanently
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Switch off lights, heaters and engines when idle

    Switching off saves fuel and money and cuts emissions.

    Source: GB F-gas rules · energy saving

Revision notes

Waste duty of care

Under the Environmental Protection Act 1990, anyone who produces, stores, carries or disposes of waste has a duty of care. Keep waste secure so it cannot escape or be blown away, pass it only to a registered waste carrier, make sure it goes to a site allowed to take it, and complete a waste transfer note describing it. Keep transfer notes for at least two years. Fly-tipping is a crime, and a business can be held responsible if waste it handed over is dumped. Do not burn waste on site.

The waste hierarchy

The waste hierarchy ranks options from best to worst: prevent waste in the first place, prepare items for reuse, recycle, recover other value such as energy, and only then dispose of what is left, for example to landfill. On electrical jobs this means ordering accurately, measuring cable runs before cutting, reusing offcuts and containment where suitable, sending packaging back where suppliers take it, and separating metals, cardboard, plastics and other waste into the right skips so more can be recycled.

Hazardous waste

Some waste is hazardous and must never go in a general skip. On electrical work this includes fluorescent and other discharge lamps containing mercury; lead-acid, nickel-cadmium and mercury batteries; oils, solvents, some paints and adhesives; contaminated rags; and asbestos. Keep all batteries out of general waste. Older equipment can hold other hazards, such as PCBs in capacitors from before the mid-1980s. Keep hazardous waste separate and labelled, store it so it cannot leak or break, and use a registered carrier and permitted site, with the correct consignment paperwork.

WEEE

The Waste Electrical and Electronic Equipment (WEEE) Regulations 2013 aim to keep electrical waste out of landfill and recover its materials. Products carry a crossed-out wheelie bin symbol to show they must be collected separately, not put in general waste. Old luminaires, lamps, switchgear, consumer units and electronic devices removed during a job should go through an authorised WEEE route. New cable offcuts are not WEEE: recycle them as scrap metal. Some producers and distributors offer take-back schemes for the items they supply.

Pollution and spills

Surface water drains usually carry rainwater straight to streams and rivers without treatment, so never let oil, fuel, paint, chemicals or cement washings get into them. Store fuels and oils in bunded containers or on drip trays, with secondary containment able to hold at least 110% of the largest container. Know where the spill kit is. If a spill happens, stop the source if it is safe to do so, contain it, protect drains and report it. The Environment Agency, SEPA, Natural Resources Wales and NIEA enforce pollution law.

Nuisance, neighbours and wildlife

Construction noise, dust, vibration, mud on roads and bright lights can all become a statutory nuisance to neighbours. Local authorities can restrict working hours and methods. Use quieter tools and methods, keep to agreed hours, damp down dust and switch off engines when not in use. Wildlife is protected too: nesting birds and bats, which often roost in roof spaces and wall cavities, must not be disturbed. If you find bats or an active nest where you are working, stop and get advice.

Saving energy and greenhouse gases

Switch off temporary lighting, heaters, tools and site cabins when they are not needed, and do not leave vehicles or generators running for no reason. Good electrical design and controls save energy for the life of a building. Some switchgear contains sulphur hexafluoride (SF6), and air conditioning and heat pumps may use fluorinated gases. These are powerful greenhouse gases, so they must not be released; only properly certified people may handle or recover them.
