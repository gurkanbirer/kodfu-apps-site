Reporting accidents: free ECS HS&E practice questions

RIDDOR, the accident book, near misses, dangerous occurrences and first-aid provision.

10 original questions from the Sparkwise app, each with the answer and an explanation. Closed book, like the real assessment: answer first, then open the explanation. No sign-up, nothing saved. These are not the real exam questions: no app or website can show you those.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    Why should near misses be reported?

    1. So the site can claim on its insurance
    2. So the cause can be fixed before someone is hurt
    3. Because HSE must be told about every one
    4. So the person involved can be found and disciplined
    Show the answer

    Answer B: So the cause can be fixed before someone is hurt

    A near miss shows a weakness in controls. Fixing it now prevents a future injury.

    Source: HSE: Investigating accidents (HSG245)

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    You are injured at work. Who should you report it to first?

    1. Your trade union office
    2. HSE, using the RIDDOR line
    3. Nobody, if you can still work
    4. Your supervisor or manager
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Your supervisor or manager

    Your supervisor can arrange first aid, record the injury and start any reporting or investigation. Reporting to HSE is the responsible person's job, not yours.

    Source: HSE: Investigating accidents (HSG245)

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    An apprentice cuts his hand on sharp trunking. What belongs in the accident book entry?

    1. The apprentice's bank details for sick pay
    2. Who you think was to blame for it
    3. How it happened and what the injury was
    4. The cost of the trunking that was damaged
    Show the answer

    Answer C: How it happened and what the injury was

    Entries record the facts: who, when, where, how it happened and the injury.

    Source: Social Security (Claims and Payments) Regs 1979

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    Under RIDDOR, who normally has the duty to make a report about an injured employee?

    1. The injured worker, as soon as possible
    2. The hospital that treats the worker
    3. The responsible person, usually the employer
    4. The police, after they attend the scene
    Show the answer

    Answer C: The responsible person, usually the employer

    For employees the employer is the responsible person. The self-employed and those in control of premises have duties in other cases.

    Source: RIDDOR 2013 regs 4-8

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    A worker breaks a finger at work. Is this a specified injury under RIDDOR?

    1. Yes, but only if it needs an operation
    2. Yes, every fracture of any bone is a specified injury
    3. No, but report it if off normal work over 7 days
    4. No, and it never needs to be reported
    Show the answer

    Answer C: No, but report it if off normal work over 7 days

    Fractures of fingers, thumbs and toes are excluded from specified injuries, but an over-seven-day absence still makes it reportable.

    Source: RIDDOR 2013 regs 4-8

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    A worker is off normal work for five days after an accident. What does RIDDOR require?

    1. It must be reported to HSE within 24 hours
    2. It must be reported to the police
    3. Nothing; it is too minor to record
    4. It must be recorded, but not reported
    Show the answer

    Answer D: It must be recorded, but not reported

    Over-three-day injuries must be recorded by the employer. They only become reportable after more than seven days.

    Source: RIDDOR 2013 reg 12

  7. Question 7 · Choose one answer

    For how long must RIDDOR records be kept?

    1. Until the job ends
    2. At least 3 years
    3. At least 3 months
    4. At least 1 year
    Show the answer

    Answer B: At least 3 years

    Records of reportable incidents and over-three-day injuries must be kept for at least three years.

    Source: RIDDOR 2013 reg 12

  8. Question 8 · Choose one answer

    Which electrical incident is a reportable dangerous occurrence?

    1. A short circuit fire stopping the plant for over 24 hours
    2. A circuit breaker tripping when a kettle is switched on
    3. An RCD tripping during a routine test of the board
    4. A lamp failing in a corridor and being replaced
    Show the answer

    Answer A: A short circuit fire stopping the plant for over 24 hours

    Electrical incidents causing fire or explosion with plant stopped for over 24 hours must be reported.

    Source: RIDDOR 2013 reg 7 and Sch 2

  9. Question 9 · Choose one answer

    Which regulations require employers to provide first-aid equipment and people?

    1. Health and Safety (First-Aid) Regulations 1981
    2. Electricity at Work Regulations 1989
    3. Provision and Use of Work Equipment Regs 1998
    4. Control of Substances Hazardous to Health Regs
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Health and Safety (First-Aid) Regulations 1981

    The First-Aid Regulations require adequate and appropriate first-aid equipment, facilities and people.

    Source: Health and Safety (First-Aid) Regulations 1981

  10. Question 10 · Choose one answer

    You use the last large dressing in the site first-aid kit. What should you do?

    1. Nothing, as the kit is checked once every year
    2. Buy one yourself and keep it in your van
    3. Tell the person responsible so it is restocked
    4. Remove the kit so nobody relies on it
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Tell the person responsible so it is restocked

    Kits must be kept stocked. Letting the responsible person know means the next casualty is not let down.

    Source: Health and Safety (First-Aid) Regulations 1981

Revision notes

Why report accidents and near misses

Reporting helps stop the same thing happening again. Tell your supervisor about every accident, however minor, and about every near miss: an event that could have caused injury or damage but by luck did not, such as a tool dropping from height and landing beside someone. Report hazards you spot, too, such as a damaged lead or a missing guardrail. Near misses are free lessons; if they are ignored, the next one may end in serious injury.

The accident book

Employers with ten or more employees, and owners of certain premises, must keep an accident book or equivalent record. Each entry should give the injured person's details, the date, time and place, how it happened and the injury. Record even minor injuries: the record can matter later if the injury turns out worse than it seemed or leads to a claim, and patterns of small injuries show where controls are weak. Entries are personal information and must be kept confidential.

RIDDOR: what must be reported

The Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013 (RIDDOR) require the 'responsible person', usually the employer, the self-employed person or whoever controls the premises, to report certain events to the enforcing authority. These include work-related deaths, specified injuries such as most fractures, amputations, serious burns and loss of sight, injuries that keep a worker off normal duties for more than seven days, members of the public taken to hospital for treatment, certain occupational diseases and listed dangerous occurrences.

RIDDOR time limits and records

Deaths and specified injuries must be reported without delay; there is an online form and a telephone line for fatal and specified injuries, and the written report must follow within ten days. An injury that keeps someone off normal work for more than seven days, not counting the day of the accident, must be reported within 15 days. An over-three-day injury must be recorded but not reported. Reportable diseases are reported once a doctor's written diagnosis is received. Records must be kept for at least three years.

Dangerous occurrences and occupational diseases

Dangerous occurrences are listed near misses that must be reported even if nobody is hurt. Examples include the collapse or partial collapse of a scaffold more than 5 m high, plant coming into contact with or arcing from an uninsulated overhead line above 200 V, an electrical short circuit or overload causing a fire or explosion that stops plant for more than 24 hours, and the overturning or collapse of lifting equipment. Reportable occupational diseases include hand-arm vibration syndrome, carpal tunnel syndrome, occupational dermatitis, occupational asthma and occupational cancer.

First-aid arrangements

The Health and Safety (First-Aid) Regulations 1981 require employers to provide adequate first-aid equipment, facilities and people, based on an assessment of their needs. A first-aider is trained to give first aid. An appointed person, used where the risk assessment allows, takes charge of arrangements, looks after the kit and calls the emergency services. Workers must be told what the arrangements are. First-aid kits should not contain tablets or medicines. Tell the person responsible when you use items, so the kit is restocked.
