Work at height: free ECS HS&E practice questions

The Work at Height Regulations, ladders, stepladders, scaffolds, mobile towers and MEWPs.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    Under the Work at Height Regulations 2005, what counts as work at height?

    1. Only work on scaffolds, towers, roofs and ladders
    2. Only work more than 2 m above the ground
    3. Only work more than 3 m above the ground
    4. Anywhere a fall could injure you, even below ground
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Anywhere a fall could injure you, even below ground

    There is no minimum height. A fall into an excavation or through a floor opening counts too.

    Source: Work at Height Regulations 2005

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    When is a ladder a suitable choice for work at height?

    1. For any job, as ladders are quick to set up
    2. For short, light, low-risk tasks only
    3. For heavy work lasting all day
    4. Whenever no scaffold is available nearby
    Show the answer

    Answer B: For short, light, low-risk tasks only

    Ladders are a last choice for low-risk, short-duration tasks. Longer or heavier work needs a platform.

    Source: HSE: Safe use of ladders (INDG455)

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    When working from a leaning ladder, what is a simple check that you are not overreaching?

    1. Lean out until you feel the ladder move
    2. Keep both hands above your head
    3. Keep your belt buckle within the stiles
    4. Keep one foot against the wall
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Keep your belt buckle within the stiles

    If your belt buckle is outside the stiles you are leaning too far. Climb down and move the ladder.

    Source: HSE: Safe use of ladders (INDG455)

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    During a pre-use check you find a ladder with a missing foot. What should you do?

    1. Pack the stile with cardboard and use it
    2. Use it but ask someone to foot it
    3. Use it carefully on a level floor
    4. Label it as defective and report it
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Label it as defective and report it

    A damaged ladder must be taken out of use. Labelling it stops someone else picking it up.

    Source: HSE: Safe use of ladders (INDG455)

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    Which steps of a stepladder does HSE guidance say you should generally avoid standing on?

    1. The bottom two steps, as they take most load
    2. Any step below the halfway point
    3. Only the bottom step of any type
    4. Top two, or top three on swing-back types
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Top two, or top three on swing-back types

    Standing too high leaves nothing to hold and makes the stepladder unstable. Use a taller stepladder or other equipment.

    Source: HSE: Safe use of ladders (INDG455)

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    On a construction site, what is the minimum height of the main guardrail on a working platform?

    1. 750 mm
    2. 500 mm
    3. 950 mm
    4. 1200 mm
    Show the answer

    Answer C: 950 mm

    Schedule 2 of the Work at Height Regulations 2005 requires the main guardrail on construction work platforms to be at least 950 mm high, so a person cannot easily topple over it.

    Source: WAHR 2005 Schedules 2 and 7

  7. Question 7 · Choose one answer

    Which statement about moving a mobile tower is correct?

    1. It can be moved with one person on it if they hold on
    2. It can be moved by pushing at platform level
    3. It can be moved under overhead power lines without a check
    4. Never move it with people or materials on the platform
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Never move it with people or materials on the platform

    Moving an occupied tower can tip it over. Clear the platform, check the route for obstructions and overhead lines, then move it.

    Source: PASMA code of practice · WAHR 2005

  8. Question 8 · Choose one answer

    You are working from a mobile tower and the platform is not quite high enough. What should you do?

    1. Get a competent person to raise the tower
    2. Put a box on the platform and stand on it
    3. Stand a stepladder on the platform
    4. Stand on the lower guardrail
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Get a competent person to raise the tower

    Using steps or boxes on a platform puts you above the guardrails and can tip the tower.

    Source: PASMA code of practice · WAHR 2005

  9. Question 9 · Select TWO answers

    Which of these should be checked before using a MEWP? Select TWO.

    1. That the ground is firm and level enough
    2. That the basket can be overloaded if needed
    3. That the pre-use checks have been carried out
    4. That the guardrails can be removed to reach further
    Show the answer

    Answer A: That the ground is firm and level enough, C: That the pre-use checks have been carried out

    Soft ground can tip the machine, and pre-use checks find faults before work starts. Guardrails stay in place and loads stay within the safe working load.

    Source: IPAF guidance · LOLER 1998

  10. Question 10 · Choose one answer

    What is the best way to stop tools falling from height onto people below?

    1. Shout a warning before you drop anything
    2. Make sure everyone below wears a hard hat
    3. Work faster so less time is spent at height
    4. Tool lanyards and an exclusion zone below
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Tool lanyards and an exclusion zone below

    Tethering tools stops them falling and an exclusion zone keeps people out of harm's way if something does.

    Source: WAHR 2005 regs 9-10 · HSE: fragile roofs (HSG33)

Revision notes

The Work at Height Regulations 2005

Work at height means work in any place where a person could fall a distance likely to cause injury, including falling into an opening or excavation at or below ground level. There is no minimum height. Work must be properly planned, supervised and done by competent people. Follow the hierarchy: avoid work at height where you can, prevent falls by using an existing safe place or suitable equipment, and where the risk remains, minimise the distance and consequences of a fall. Collective protection such as guardrails comes before personal protection such as harnesses.

Leaning ladders

Ladders are for short, light, low-risk work where something safer is not justified. Set a leaning ladder at about 75 degrees, one unit out for every four up, on firm, level ground. It should rest against a strong surface, not guttering or plastic, and extend at least 1 m above a landing point unless there is another secure handhold. Tie it where possible. Keep three points of contact when climbing, carry tools in a belt, and keep your belt buckle within the stiles so you do not overreach.

Checking a ladder before use

Check every ladder or stepladder before use. Look for bent, split or damaged stiles, missing or worn feet, loose, bent or missing rungs or treads, and damaged locking bars. Make sure it is clean and free of contamination such as wet paint, mud or oil. Do not use a damaged ladder: label it and report it. Wooden ladders should not be painted because paint can hide cracks. For electrical work near live parts, use a non-conductive glass-fibre ladder rather than an aluminium one.

Stepladders

Open a stepladder fully and make sure any locking devices are engaged before you climb. Stand it on firm, level ground and face the work where possible. Avoid standing on the top two steps, or the top three on swing-back or double-sided stepladders where a step forms the very top, unless there is a suitable handhold. Keep a handhold or three points of contact at your working position for anything more than very short light tasks. Do not overreach or push sideways; climb down and move the stepladder instead.

Scaffolds

Only competent scaffolders may erect, alter or dismantle a scaffold. Never move boards, remove guardrails or take out ties yourself, even for a moment; ask for the change to be made. Scaffolds must be inspected before first use, at intervals of no more than seven days and after anything that could affect their stability, such as high winds. Many sites use tags to show whether a scaffold is safe to use. On construction sites, platforms need a main guardrail at least 950 mm high, an intermediate rail leaving no gap above 470 mm, and toe boards.

Mobile access towers

Mobile towers must be built by trained, competent people following the manufacturer's instructions. Use the stabilisers or outriggers supplied, level the base and lock every castor before climbing. Climb on the inside using the built-in ladder or frames, never the outside. Never move a tower with people or materials on it, and look out for overhead obstructions and power lines when moving it. Do not use ladders or steps on the platform to gain extra height. Stop work if the wind exceeds the manufacturer's limit.

Mobile elevating work platforms (MEWPs)

Only trained and authorised people should operate a MEWP, after carrying out the manufacturer's pre-use checks. Check that the ground can take the load, set up any outriggers and keep an exclusion zone below. In boom-type MEWPs, wear a full body harness with a short work restraint lanyard clipped to the anchor point in the basket. Watch for overhead hazards and the risk of being trapped between the basket and nearby steelwork or ceilings. There must be a plan to rescue someone from the platform.

Fragile surfaces, openings and falling objects

Many roofs have fragile surfaces, such as old cement sheets and rooflights, that will not take a person's weight. Never walk on them; use staging with guardrails, covers or safety nets. Holes and openings in floors must be protected by guardrails or by covers that are strong, fixed in place and clearly marked. Falling tools and materials can kill people below, so use toe boards, tool lanyards and exclusion zones, and never throw materials down: use a chute or hoist.
