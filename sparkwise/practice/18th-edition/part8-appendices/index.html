  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    In BS 7671:2018+A4:2026, which chapter covers prosumer's low-voltage electrical installations?

    1. Chapter 57
    2. Chapter 81
    3. Chapter 82
    4. Section 712
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Chapter 82

    Chapter 82 covers prosumer's low-voltage electrical installations. Chapter 81 covers energy efficiency and Chapter 57 batteries.

    Where to look: Part 8 · Chapter 82

  2. Question 2 · True or false?

    Chapter 81 requires every new installation to include specific technologies, such as solar PV and battery storage.

    1. True
    2. False
    Show the answer

    Answer B: False

    Chapter 81 is informative. It points to the energy requirements of the Building Regulations and to BS HD 60364-8-1. It does not require any particular technology, such as solar PV or batteries.

    Where to look: Chapter 81 (A4)

  3. Question 3 · Select TWO answers

    Chapter 82 recognises which TWO operating modes for a PEI? Select TWO.

    1. Connected to the public network
    2. Island mode, off the public network
    3. Standby mode with export blocked
    4. Bypass mode with RCDs shorted out
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Connected to the public network, B: Island mode, off the public network

    A PEI may operate connected to the public network or in island mode. Protection must be effective in both.

    Where to look: Chapter 82

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    Which appendix lists the British Standards referred to in BS 7671?

    1. Appendix 1
    2. Appendix 2
    3. Appendix 6
    4. Appendix 16
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Appendix 1

    Appendix 1 lists the standards referred to. Appendix 2 lists statutory regulations.

    Where to look: Appendix 1

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    You need to know what the external influence code AF4 means. Which appendix would you use?

    1. Appendix 2
    2. Appendix 5
    3. Appendix 6
    4. Appendix 9
    Show the answer

    Answer B: Appendix 5

    Appendix 5 classifies external influences. AF codes relate to corrosive or polluting substances.

    Where to look: Appendix 5

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    In an old three-phase installation, the conductors are red, yellow and blue. Which harmonised colours correspond to yellow and blue?

    1. Yellow → black, blue → grey
    2. Yellow → grey, blue → black
    3. Yellow → brown, blue → blue
    4. Yellow → blue, blue → grey
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Yellow → black, blue → grey

    Red, yellow and blue map to brown, black and grey. Old blue L3 is now grey, so blue must not be mistaken for neutral.

    Where to look: Table 51 · Reg 514.14

  7. Question 7 · Choose one answer

    Which appendix explains the effect of harmonic currents on balanced three-phase systems?

    1. Appendix 4
    2. Appendix 8
    3. Appendix 11
    4. Appendix 16
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Appendix 4

    Appendix 4 (section 5.5) explains how triple-n harmonics can raise neutral current and which rating factors to apply. Appendix 11 now gives examples of warning and instruction labels.

    Where to look: Appendix 4 §5.5

  8. Question 8 · Choose one answer

    According to Appendix 15, what may a single non-fused spur from a ring final circuit supply?

    1. Up to three single socket-outlets
    2. One single or twin socket, or one item
    3. Two twin sockets in the same room
    4. Any number of sockets in one room
    Show the answer

    Answer B: One single or twin socket, or one item

    A non-fused spur may feed only one single or twin/multiple socket-outlet, or one item of permanently connected equipment.

    Where to look: Appendix 15

  9. Question 9 · Choose one answer

    Where in BS 7671 would you look for typical ring and radial final circuit arrangements?

    1. Appendix 4
    2. Appendix 10
    3. Appendix 14
    4. Appendix 15
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Appendix 15

    Appendix 15 shows ring and radial final circuit arrangements.

    Where to look: Appendix 15

  10. Question 10 · Choose one answer

    A fault current of 400 A flows through a 32 A Type B circuit-breaker. Using its time/current characteristic, how does it respond?

    1. It trips instantly, as 400 A exceeds 160 A
    2. It takes up to 5 s, as 400 A is below 10 × In
    3. It does not trip, as 400 A is below 20 × In
    4. It trips only once the cable overheats
    Show the answer

    Answer A: It trips instantly, as 400 A exceeds 160 A

    Type B devices trip in the instantaneous region at 5 × In or more. 5 × 32 = 160 A, and 400 A is well above it.

    Worked solution. Formula: Instantaneous if I ≥ Ia (Type B: Ia = 5 × In)

    1. Find the instantaneous threshold: 5 × 32 = 160 A
    2. Compare with the fault current: 400 A is more than 160 A
    3. Read the curve: The device operates in its instantaneous region, well within 0.1 s

    Common mistake: Using the Type C or D multiplier (10 or 20 × In) wrongly suggests a slow trip.

    Where to look: Appendix 3 · time/current curves

Revision notes

What Part 8 contains

Part 8 sets functional requirements rather than safety rules alone. In BS 7671:2018+A4:2026 it holds Chapter 81 on energy efficiency, which A4 brought into the body of the standard in place of the old Appendix 17, and Chapter 82 on prosumer's low-voltage electrical installations, first added by Amendment 2. Questions in the exam on Part 8 usually ask what these chapters cover and how they relate to the safety requirements in Parts 4 and 5.

Energy efficiency (Chapter 81)

Chapter 81 on energy efficiency is informative: it is there for information and says the legal requirements for conserving energy are in the Building Regulations (Section 6 of the Technical Handbooks in Scotland). BS HD 60364-8-1 gives recommendations, such as placing distribution equipment near the load centre, sizing conductors on long, heavily loaded runs to cut losses, choosing efficient equipment and providing metering. Chapter 81 does not require any particular technology, and efficiency must never reduce safety.

Prosumer's installations (Chapter 82)

A prosumer's electrical installation (PEI) includes local sources such as PV or generators and often storage, so it can both take energy from and supply energy to the network. It may operate connected to the public network or in island mode, disconnected from it. An electrical energy management system (EEMS) controls sources, storage and loads. The designer must make sure protection, including earthing and automatic disconnection, still works in every operating mode and during changeover between them.

Appendices 1 to 4

Appendix 1 lists the British and other standards the Regulations refer to. Appendix 2 lists statutory regulations and associated memoranda, such as the Electricity at Work Regulations 1989, the ESQCR 2002 and Building Regulations. Appendix 3 gives time/current characteristics of overcurrent protective devices (its RCD table was removed in Amendment 2), which are used to find the current Ia that operates a device within a given time. Appendix 4 gives current-carrying capacities, voltage drop figures in mV/A/m, installation reference methods and rating factors for cables.

Appendices 5 to 7

Appendix 5 sets out the full classification of external influences, such as AD for water and BE for stored materials. Appendix 6 contains the model forms for certification and reporting: the Electrical Installation Certificate, the Minor Electrical Installation Works Certificate, the Electrical Installation Condition Report, and their schedules and guidance for recipients. The former Appendix 7 on old cable colours was deleted by Amendment 2; Table 51 and Section 514 now cover colours and the mixed-colour notice.

Appendices 8 to 16

The later appendices support specific design tasks. Appendix 8 covers current-carrying capacity and voltage drop for busbar trunking and powertrack. Appendix 9 covers multiple-source, DC and other systems. Appendix 10 covers protection of conductors in parallel against overcurrent. Appendix 11 gives examples of warning and instruction labels. Appendix 13 gives guidance on escape routes and fire protection, supporting Chapter 42. Appendix 14 covers determining prospective fault current. Appendix 15 shows ring and radial final circuit arrangements. Appendix 16 covers devices for protection against overvoltage. Harmonic currents in the neutral are dealt with in Appendix 4.
