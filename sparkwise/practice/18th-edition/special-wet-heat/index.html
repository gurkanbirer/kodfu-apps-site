  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    What is the purpose of Section 700 in Part 7?

    1. It replaces Parts 1 to 6 for all special locations
    2. It introduces Part 7, which modifies the general rules
    3. It covers only medical and veterinary locations
    4. It lists the model forms for special locations
    Show the answer

    Answer B: It introduces Part 7, which modifies the general rules

    Part 7 sections add to or change the general rules for special locations. Where Part 7 says nothing, the general rules still apply.

    Where to look: Section 700

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    What is the height limit of zone 1 above the finished floor, unless a fixed shower head is higher?

    1. 2.0 m
    2. 2.25 m
    3. 2.5 m
    4. 3.0 m
    Show the answer

    Answer B: 2.25 m

    Zone 1 extends to 2.25 m above the finished floor level, or to the highest fixed shower head or water outlet if that is higher.

    Where to look: Reg 701.32.3

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    For a walk-in shower without a basin, how far does zone 1 extend horizontally from the centre of the fixed water outlet?

    1. 0.6 m
    2. 1.2 m
    3. 2.25 m
    4. 3 m
    Show the answer

    Answer B: 1.2 m

    Without a shower tray, zone 1 extends 1.2 m horizontally from the centre of the fixed water outlet on the wall or ceiling.

    Where to look: Reg 701.32.3

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    What minimum IP rating is required for equipment in zone 0 of a bathroom?

    1. IPX4
    2. IPX5
    3. IPX7
    4. IP2X
    Show the answer

    Answer C: IPX7

    Zone 0 equipment may be immersed, so it must be at least IPX7.

    Where to look: Reg 701.512.2

  5. Question 5 · Select TWO answers

    Which TWO types of socket-outlet may be installed within 2.5 m of the zone 1 boundary? Select TWO.

    1. A shaver supply unit
    2. A SELV socket-outlet
    3. A 13 A socket with 30 mA RCD
    4. An industrial BS EN 60309 socket
    Show the answer

    Answer A: A shaver supply unit, B: A SELV socket-outlet

    Only SELV socket-outlets and shaver supply units with an isolating transformer to BS EN 61558-2-5 are allowed there.

    Where to look: Reg 701.512.3

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    Which circuits in a bathroom need 30 mA RCD additional protection?

    1. Only the shower and other zone 1 equipment
    2. All LV circuits serving it or passing through
    3. Only circuits feeding socket-outlets nearby
    4. Only the lighting and extractor fan circuits
    Show the answer

    Answer B: All LV circuits serving it or passing through

    Every low-voltage circuit serving the location, and any passing through zones 1 or 2, needs 30 mA RCD protection.

    Where to look: Reg 701.411.3.3

  7. Question 7 · Choose one answer

    How far does zone 1 of a swimming pool extend horizontally from the rim of the basin?

    1. 0.6 m
    2. 1.5 m
    3. 2 m
    4. 3.5 m
    Show the answer

    Answer C: 2 m

    Zone 1 of a pool extends 2 m from the rim, up to 2.5 m above the surrounding floor.

    Where to look: Reg 702.32

  8. Question 8 · Choose one answer

    Where must the safety source for SELV equipment in zone 0 of a pool be located?

    1. Inside zone 0 next to the equipment
    2. Inside zone 1 at the poolside
    3. Outside zones 0, 1 and 2
    4. Anywhere within the pool hall
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Outside zones 0, 1 and 2

    The SELV source must be outside zones 0, 1 and 2 so it is not exposed to the wet conditions.

    Where to look: Reg 702.410.3.4 · Section 702

  9. Question 9 · Choose one answer

    What must the installer of embedded heating give to the user?

    1. A spare length of the same heating cable
    2. A plan showing where the heating units are
    3. A copy of the installer's insurance policy
    4. A spare key for the room thermostat
    Show the answer

    Answer B: A plan showing where the heating units are

    A plan and description help prevent later damage, such as drilling into heating cables, and support maintenance.

    Where to look: Reg 753.514

  10. Question 10 · Choose one answer

    Where in BS 7671 would you look for the requirements for underfloor heating cables?

    1. Section 554
    2. Section 701
    3. Section 703
    4. Section 753
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Section 753

    Section 753 covers heating cables and embedded heating systems.

    Where to look: Section 753

Revision notes

How Part 7 works

Part 7 sets extra or modified requirements for special installations and locations where the risk is higher. Each section supplements or changes the general rules in Parts 1 to 6; where Part 7 is silent, the general rules still apply. Numbering follows the general rule it modifies: for example 701.411.3.3 is the bathroom version of 411.3.3 on additional protection. Section 700 introduces Part 7 and explains this structure.

Bathroom zones

Section 701 divides locations containing a bath or shower into zones. Zone 0 is the inside of the bath or shower tray. Zone 1 is directly above zone 0 up to 2.25 m above the finished floor, or to the highest fixed shower head if higher. For a shower without a tray, zone 1 extends 1.2 m horizontally from the centre of the fixed water outlet. Zone 2 extends a further 0.6 m horizontally beyond zone 1, to the same height. The space under a bath counts as outside the zones only if it can be reached just with a tool.

Bathroom equipment and IP ratings

Equipment in zone 0 must be at least IPX7 and supplied by SELV not exceeding 12 V AC or 30 V ripple-free DC, with the source outside the zones. Equipment in zones 1 and 2 must be at least IPX4, or IPX5 where water jets are used for cleaning. Only fixed, permanently connected equipment suitable for the zone may go in zone 1, such as showers, whirlpool units, fans and towel rails. Socket-outlets are not allowed within 2.5 m horizontally of the zone 1 boundary, except SELV sockets and shaver supply units to BS EN 61558-2-5.

Bathroom RCDs and bonding

All low-voltage circuits serving the location, and those passing through zones 1 or 2 even if they do not serve the room, must have additional protection by a 30 mA RCD. Supplementary bonding may be left out if every circuit of the location meets its disconnection time, all are protected by a 30 mA RCD, and all extraneous-conductive-parts of the location are effectively connected to the main protective bonding. Where it is needed, it joins the cpcs of circuits and the extraneous-conductive-parts within the location.

Swimming pools and other basins

Section 702 uses zones too. Zone 0 is the inside of the basin. Zone 1 extends 2 m horizontally from the rim and up to 2.5 m above the surrounding floor. Zone 2 extends a further 1.5 m beyond zone 1. Minimum ratings are IPX8 in zone 0 and IPX4 in zone 1 (IPX5 where jets clean the area); zone 2 needs IPX2 indoors and IPX4 outdoors. Equipment in zones 0 and 1 is generally SELV at no more than 12 V AC or 30 V DC, with the source outside zones 0, 1 and 2.

Saunas

Section 703 covers rooms and cabins with sauna heaters. The hot room is divided into zones by distance from the heater and height, with the upper zone exposed to the highest temperatures, so equipment there must withstand 125 °C and cable insulation and sheaths 170 °C. Equipment needs at least IPX4, or IPX5 where cleaning with water jets is expected. All circuits of the sauna need 30 mA RCD protection except the sauna heater itself, unless its manufacturer recommends it. Only the sauna's own equipment should be inside the hot room.

Heating cables and embedded heating

Section 753 covers heating cables and embedded heating systems in floors, walls and ceilings, and surface heating such as de-icing. Heating circuits need additional protection by an RCD of 30 mA or less. Heating units without an exposed metal sheath or grid need a metal grid or covering connected to the protective conductor. The installer must give the user a description and a plan showing where the heating units are, so nobody later drills or fixes into them.
