  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    In domestic premises, what does BS 7671 require of a consumer unit enclosure?

    1. It must be made from flame-retardant plastic
    2. Made of, or housed in, non-combustible material
    3. It must be rated IP65 or better indoors
    4. It must be mounted outside the dwelling
    Show the answer

    Answer B: Made of, or housed in, non-combustible material

    Consumer units in domestic premises need a non-combustible enclosure, or must sit in a non-combustible cabinet, to contain a fire.

    Where to look: Reg 421.1.201

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    For a socket-outlet circuit in an ordinary two-storey house, what does BS 7671 say about AFDDs?

    1. They are prohibited
    2. They are required
    3. They are recommended
    4. They are only allowed with Type B RCDs
    Show the answer

    Answer C: They are recommended

    Outside the four listed premises types, AFDDs are recommended for such circuits rather than required.

    Where to look: Reg 421.1.7

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    Which section deals with locations constructed of combustible materials, such as timber buildings?

    1. Section 421
    2. Section 422
    3. Section 433
    4. Section 443
    Show the answer

    Answer B: Section 422

    Section 422 covers particular fire risks, including combustible constructional materials (CA2) and fire-propagating structures (CB2).

    Where to look: Reg 422.4

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    In the relationship Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz, what does Iz represent?

    1. The design current of the circuit
    2. The rated current of the device
    3. The cable's current-carrying capacity
    4. The prospective fault current
    Show the answer

    Answer C: The cable's current-carrying capacity

    Iz is the current the conductor can carry continuously in its actual conditions. Ib is the design current and In the device rating.

    Where to look: Reg 433.1.1

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    Where must an overload protective device normally be placed?

    1. At the far end of each circuit run
    2. Where the cable's capacity is reduced
    3. Next to the largest load on the circuit
    4. At least 3 m from the point of supply
    Show the answer

    Answer B: Where the cable's capacity is reduced

    A device goes where Iz is reduced, for example by a change in cross-sectional area, installation method or environment.

    Where to look: Reg 433.2.1

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    A circuit-breaker's breaking capacity is lower than the prospective fault current at its position. When is this acceptable?

    1. Never, in any circumstances
    2. With coordinated back-up from upstream
    3. When the circuit is under 10 m long
    4. When the circuit also has an RCD
    Show the answer

    Answer B: With coordinated back-up from upstream

    Lower breaking capacity is allowed only with back-up protection from an upstream device that can break the fault and limits energy to what the downstream devices withstand.

    Where to look: Reg 434.5.1

  7. Question 7 · Choose one answer

    A motor circuit has a high inrush current that keeps tripping a Type B device. Which type of circuit-breaker is generally better suited?

    1. Type A
    2. Type B of lower rating
    3. Type C or D
    4. Type AC
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Type C or D

    Type C and D need higher multiples of In to trip instantly, so they ride through inrush. Check the lower maximum Zs that results.

    Where to look: BS EN 60898 · Types C and D

  8. Question 8 · Choose one answer

    Where in BS 7671 would you look for precautions for locations with combustible stored materials (BE2)?

    1. Section 421
    2. Section 422
    3. Section 423
    4. Section 424
    Show the answer

    Answer B: Section 422

    Section 422 covers locations with particular fire risks, including BE2, CA2 and CB2.

    Where to look: Section 422

  9. Question 9 · Choose one answer

    A 20 A circuit-breaker (I2 = 1.45 × In) protects a cable with Iz of 21 A. Is I2 ≤ 1.45 × Iz satisfied?

    1. Yes, 29.0 A ≤ 30.5 A
    2. No, 29.0 A > 21.0 A
    3. No, 30.5 A > 29.0 A
    4. Yes, 20.0 A ≤ 21.0 A
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Yes, 29.0 A ≤ 30.5 A

    I2 = 1.45 × 20 = 29 A and 1.45 × Iz = 1.45 × 21 = 30.45 A, so the condition holds. Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz is a separate check.

    Worked solution. Formula: I2 ≤ 1.45 × Iz

    1. Find I2: For this device I2 = 1.45 × In = 1.45 × 20 = 29.0 A
    2. Find the cable limit: 1.45 × Iz = 1.45 × 21 = 30.45 A
    3. Compare: 29.0 A is less than 30.45 A, so it complies

    Common mistake: Comparing I2 directly with Iz (29 A against 21 A) wrongly suggests a failure.

    Where to look: Reg 433.1.1

  10. Question 10 · Choose one answer

    Under A4, which of these may be installed in a fire-fighting shaft or a protected stairway of a protected escape route?

    1. Sockets for cleaning or maintenance
    2. A distribution board for the flats above
    3. Sub-main cables to individual flats
    4. Electric storage heaters for comfort
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Sockets for cleaning or maintenance

    New Regulation 422.2.201 keeps cables and equipment out of fire-fighting shafts and protected stairways unless they serve an essential fire safety system, general needs lighting or socket-outlets for cleaning and maintenance. Fire-rated cables in a suitable fire-resisting enclosure may be acceptable under 422.2.202, being treated as outside the route.

    Where to look: Reg 422.2.201 · Reg 422.2.202 (A4)

Revision notes

Fire and heat from fixed equipment

Chapter 42 protects people, livestock and property from the heat electrical equipment produces. Fixed equipment must be chosen and installed so its temperature cannot ignite nearby materials: mount it on or in materials that withstand the heat, screen it, or keep a safe distance. Equipment that focuses heat, such as spotlights, needs enough distance from combustible surfaces. In domestic premises, consumer units and similar switchgear assemblies must be to BS EN 61439-3 and have a non-combustible enclosure, or be housed in a non-combustible cabinet.

Arc fault detection devices

Regulation 421.1.7 requires AFDDs to BS EN 62606 on single-phase AC final circuits supplying socket-outlets rated up to 32 A in high-rise residential buildings (HRRBs), houses in multiple occupation, purpose-built student accommodation and care homes. In all other premises they are recommended for such circuits, not required. Amendment 4 changed the wording from 'higher risk' to 'high-rise' residential buildings without changing the requirement. For this purpose an HRRB is one over 18 m tall or with more than six storeys.

Locations with particular fire risks

Section 422 adds precautions where fire risk is higher. Where evacuation would be difficult (BD codes), wiring in escape routes is limited and protected. In locations with combustible processed or stored materials (BE2), such as barns or woodworking shops, wiring other than mineral insulated cable, busbar trunking or powertrack must be protected by an RCD of no more than 300 mA, and 30 mA where resistive faults could start a fire, such as heating films. Combustible construction (CA2) and fire-propagating structures (CB2) have their own rules.

Burns and overheating

Table 42.1 limits the temperature of accessible parts within arm's reach. Hand-held parts may reach 55 °C if metallic and 65 °C if non-metallic. Parts touched but not held may reach 70 °C (metallic) and 80 °C (non-metallic). Parts that need not be touched in normal use may reach 80 °C and 90 °C. Section 424 covers overheating: forced air heaters must not heat until airflow is established and need independent temperature limiters, and appliances producing hot water or steam need protection against overheating.

Overload protection

Chapter 43 requires protective devices to break any overload before it damages insulation, joints or surroundings. The basic rule is Ib ≤ In ≤ Iz: design current no more than the device rating, which is no more than the cable's current-carrying capacity. The device must also operate reliably at I2 ≤ 1.45 × Iz. A semi-enclosed fuse to BS 3036 needs In ≤ 0.725 × Iz. A 2.5 mm² ring final circuit on a 30 A or 32 A device is accepted if the cable's Iz is at least 20 A.

Position and omission of overload devices

An overload device normally goes where the current-carrying capacity falls, for example where the cable size, installation method or conditions change. It may be placed further along if no branch circuits or outlets sit between, or up to 3 m along if the risk of fault, fire and danger is minimised. Overload protection may be omitted where breaking the circuit would itself be dangerous, such as circuits for lifting magnets, exciter circuits of machines, current transformer secondaries and fire extinguishing equipment.

Fault current protection

The prospective fault current must be determined at every relevant point. Each device must be able to break the maximum fault current at its position, unless an upstream device with enough breaking capacity backs it up and the two are coordinated. The fault must also be cleared before the conductors overheat; the adiabatic relationship t = k²S²/I² applies for times up to 5 s. Domestic consumer units to BS EN 61439-3 have a conditional short-circuit rating, commonly 16 kA, when protected by a suitable supply cut-out fuse.

Circuit-breakers, fuses and selectivity

Circuit-breakers to BS EN 60898 come in Types B, C and D. A Type B trips instantaneously between 3 and 5 times In, a Type C between 5 and 10 times, and a Type D between 10 and 20 times, so Type C and D suit high inrush loads. Fuses include BS 88-2, BS 88-3 and the older BS 3036 rewirable type. Selectivity, formerly called discrimination, means only the device nearest a fault operates, leaving the rest of the installation working.
