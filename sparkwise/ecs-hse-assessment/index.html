ECS Health, Safety and Environmental assessment guide

The ECS HS&E assessment is the health and safety test for people who work in the electrotechnical industry. You need a pass, or a valid exemption, to apply for an ECS card. It is 50 multiple-choice questions in 30 minutes, and you need 43 right. This guide covers who needs it, what it covers, how to book and how to prepare.

Who needs the ECS HS&E assessment?

The Electrotechnical Certification Scheme (ECS) is the card scheme for the electrotechnical industry, run by the JIB. Many employers and sites ask for an ECS card to show that you are qualified and safety-aware. To apply for an ECS card you must either pass the ECS Health, Safety and Environmental assessment or hold a valid exemption. The ECS says it sets a minimum safety standard for cardholders.

That covers most people starting out or moving up in the trade: apprentices and trainees, electricians and installers, and other electrotechnical workers. Some higher or equivalent qualifications count as an exemption; the ECS website lists which ones. See the ECS HS&E assessment page for the card types and exemptions that apply to you.

In Scotland, ECS applications and information are handled by the Scottish Joint Industry Board (SJIB).

Format and pass mark

ItemECS HS&E assessment
Questions50 multiple choice, one correct answer from four
Time30 minutes
Pass mark43 out of 50 (86%)
Book or notesNone: closed book
HowOnline with remote invigilation, or face to face at a venue

The pass mark is high. You can only get seven questions wrong, so a "mostly right" level of knowledge is not enough. Thirty minutes for 50 questions is a little over half a minute each, so there is no time to puzzle over every one. Aim to know the material well enough to answer most questions straight away.

Questions are drawn from a bank the ECS publishes in its own revision book, which you can download from the ECS website's preparation and revision section. That makes the official revision book the first thing to read.

Syllabus areas

The assessment covers 11 areas. The ECS revision guide sets out how many questions come from each; at the time of writing the split was:

AreaQuestionsWhat it covers
General health and safety at work6Health and Safety at Work etc. Act duties, enforcement, risk assessments, method statements, permits, CDM, safety signs
Manual handling4Avoid, assess, reduce; safe lifting; handling aids
Reporting accidents3RIDDOR, the accident book, near misses, first aid
Personal protective equipment4Who provides PPE, choosing and caring for it, PPE as the last resort
Health and hygiene3COSHH, dust, noise, vibration, skin problems, Weil's disease, welfare
Fire and emergency9Fire triangle, extinguishers, fire stopping, hot work, evacuation, first aid, electric shock
Work at height5Work at Height Regulations, ladders, stepladders, towers, scaffolds, MEWPs
Work equipment4PUWER, hand and power tools, 110 V site supplies, lifting equipment
Special site hazards3Asbestos, buried services, overhead lines, excavations, confined spaces
Electrotechnical safety6Electricity at Work Regulations, safe isolation, test equipment, lock-off, live working
Environmental3Waste duty of care, WEEE, hazardous waste, spills, nuisance

Fire and emergency is the biggest area, with almost a fifth of the questions. Check the current revision guide in case the split has changed.

How to book and sit it

Online, with remote invigilation

You can sit the assessment at home, at work or anywhere else private, with a webcam and a suitable internet connection. Monitoring software uses your webcam, microphone, keyboard and mouse and checks your identity. If it detects unusual activity, the assessment is failed automatically, so sit alone, clear your desk and close other programs. Bookings are made through your MyECS account or by your employer through the ECS Employer Portal; the ECS website explains the current route and the setup checks to run first.

Face to face

Face-to-face sessions run through selected employers and training providers in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, listed on the ECS venues page. In Scotland, contact the SJIB.

Cost

The ECS lists its fees on its website, and some providers add their own charge for running the assessment, so prices vary. Check the current fee with the ECS or your provider before you book. The ECS warns against third-party booking agents who charge extra: you do not need an agent to book the assessment or apply for a card.

Help with reading

If you need text-to-speech, contact the ECS Customer Experience Centre before the assessment. For other adjustments, speak to your test provider or employer.

Results, validity and renewal

After the assessment, your MyECS account is updated or a result letter is sent to you, usually by email. Once you have passed, you continue your card application in MyECS.

A pass is valid for two years. Within that time you need to apply for your ECS card; if you leave it longer, you will need to take a new assessment. When your card comes up for renewal, check in MyECS what the renewal asks for, and book a fresh assessment in good time if you need one.

How it differs from the CITB HS&E test

People often mix up the two. The CITB Health, Safety and Environment test is the one used for CSCS cards across general construction. The ECS HS&E assessment is a separate test for the electrotechnical industry and leads to an ECS card.

  • Not interchangeable: the ECS does not accept the CITB (CSCS) test. It says its own assessment covers the safety needs of electrotechnical workers, including fire safety.
  • Different content: the ECS assessment has its own electrotechnical safety section (safe isolation, test equipment, live working) and a large fire and emergency section.
  • Different format and booking: the CITB test has its own question count, time limit and pass mark and is booked through CITB. Do not use CITB revision material as your main source for the ECS assessment.

How to prepare

  1. Download the ECS revision book from the ECS website and read it through once, area by area.
  2. Practise by topic. Start with the big areas: fire and emergency, general health and safety, electrotechnical safety and work at height.
  3. Learn the reasons, not just the answers. Know why safe isolation follows the order it does, why CO2 suits electrical fires and why PPE comes last in the hierarchy. Understanding carries over to questions worded differently.
  4. Take timed mocks. Sit full 50-question, 30-minute mocks and aim to score well above 43 before booking, so a few unlucky questions on the day do not matter.
  5. Go back over your mistakes. Revisit the questions you got wrong a day or two later.
  6. Check your setup if you are sitting online: webcam, microphone, connection, a quiet private room and the ECS system checks.
