Life in the UK practice: Chapter 1, the values and principles of the UK

Free, with no sign-up and nothing saved. These 10 questions are original, written from the facts in the handbook. No app or website can show you the real test questions.

What this chapter covers

Chapter 1 sets out the ideas that British life is built on and what is expected of people who make their home here. It is less about dates than about what each idea means in practice, so these questions ask you to apply the principles as well as name them.

  • The fundamental principles: democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, tolerance of other faiths and beliefs, and taking part in community life.
  • The responsibilities of living in the UK, such as obeying the law, respecting other people's rights and treating others fairly.
  • The freedoms the UK offers, including freedom of belief and of speech, and the right to a fair trial.
  • Citizenship ceremonies: the oath or affirmation, and the pledge.
  • Settling in the UK: indefinite leave to remain, citizenship and who has to take the test.

Worth remembering

  • The rule of law means nobody is above the law, including people in power.
  • Free speech has limits: threatening people or stirring up hatred is not protected.
  • At a citizenship ceremony you swear an oath, which mentions God, or make an affirmation, which does not. Then you make a pledge to the UK.
  • Indefinite leave to remain lets you live here permanently. Becoming a British citizen is a separate application.
