Life in the UK practice: Chapter 1, the values and principles of the UK

Free, with no sign-up and nothing saved. These 10 questions are original, written from the facts in the handbook. No app or website can show you the real test questions.

What this chapter covers

Chapter 1 sets out the ideas that British life is built on and what is expected of people who make their home here. It is less about dates than about what each idea means in practice, so these questions ask you to apply the principles as well as name them.

  • The fundamental principles: democracy, the rule of law, individual liberty, tolerance of other faiths and beliefs, and taking part in community life.
  • The responsibilities of living in the UK, such as obeying the law, respecting other people's rights and treating others fairly.
  • The freedoms the UK offers, including freedom of belief and of speech, and the right to a fair trial.
  • Citizenship ceremonies: the oath or affirmation, and the pledge.
  • Settling in the UK: indefinite leave to remain, citizenship and who has to take the test.

  1. Question 1 · Select TWO answers

    Which of these are fundamental principles of British life? Select TWO.

    1. Following one national religion
    2. Tolerance of other faiths and beliefs
    3. Individual liberty
    4. Loyalty to one political party
    Show the answer

    Answer B: Tolerance of other faiths and beliefs, C: Individual liberty

    Individual liberty and tolerance of different faiths and beliefs are core principles. The UK has no single compulsory party or religion.

    Source: Handbook ch. 1 · The values and principles of the UK

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    Which phrase best describes the principle of individual liberty?

    1. Freedom to ignore any laws you personally disagree with
    2. A right to be given a job by the state
    3. Freedom to make your own choices within the law
    4. A duty to follow your family's religion
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Freedom to make your own choices within the law

    Individual liberty means people can choose how to live their lives, as long as they stay within the law.

    Source: Handbook ch. 1 · The values and principles of the UK

  3. Question 3 · Select TWO answers

    Which of these are responsibilities that people living in the UK should accept? Select TWO.

    1. Treating others with fairness
    2. Agreeing with every government policy
    3. Looking after yourself and your family
    4. Speaking only English in your home
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Treating others with fairness, C: Looking after yourself and your family

    Treating others fairly and looking after yourself and your family are both expected. You may disagree with the government and use any language at home.

    Source: Handbook ch. 1 · The values and principles of the UK

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    A neighbour strongly disagrees with your political views. What does British life expect you to do?

    1. Respect their right to hold their view
    2. Report them to the police
    3. Ask the council to warn them
    4. Insist that they change their view
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Respect their right to hold their view

    Everyone has the right to their own opinion, and respecting that right is one of the responsibilities of living in the UK.

    Source: Handbook ch. 1 · The values and principles of the UK

  5. Question 5 · Choose one answer

    Which right does everyone in the UK have if they are accused of a crime?

    1. The right to choose their judge
    2. The right to a fair trial
    3. The right to be tried in their country of birth
    4. The right to a trial without a judge
    Show the answer

    Answer B: The right to a fair trial

    A fair trial is one of the freedoms guaranteed to everyone living in the UK.

    Source: Handbook ch. 1 · The values and principles of the UK

  6. Question 6 · True or false?

    Freedom of speech means you can say anything in the UK, including threats of violence, without breaking the law.

    1. True
    2. False
    Show the answer

    Answer B: False

    Free speech is protected, but it works within the law. Threatening violence or stirring up hatred can be a crime.

    Source: Handbook ch. 1 · The values and principles of the UK

  7. Question 7 · Choose one answer

    At a citizenship ceremony, what is the difference between the oath and the affirmation?

    1. The affirmation is only for children
    2. The oath is spoken and the affirmation is sung
    3. The oath is made to Parliament and the affirmation to the monarch
    4. The oath mentions God and the affirmation does not
    Show the answer

    Answer D: The oath mentions God and the affirmation does not

    People who wish to swear by God take the oath. Those who prefer a non-religious promise make a solemn affirmation instead.

    Source: Handbook ch. 1 · The values and principles of the UK

  8. Question 8 · Select TWO answers

    In the citizenship pledge, new citizens promise to do which of these? Select TWO.

    1. Vote at every election
    2. Live in the UK for the rest of their lives
    3. Uphold the UK's democratic values
    4. Observe its laws faithfully
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Uphold the UK's democratic values, D: Observe its laws faithfully

    The pledge covers respecting rights and freedoms, upholding democratic values, observing the laws and fulfilling the duties of a citizen. Voting is not compulsory.

    Source: Handbook ch. 1 · The values and principles of the UK

  9. Question 9 · Choose one answer

    What does indefinite leave to remain allow a person to do?

    1. Live in the UK permanently
    2. Hold a British passport
    3. Visit the UK for up to six months
    4. Vote in every UK election
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Live in the UK permanently

    Indefinite leave to remain, often called settlement, gives permission to live in the UK permanently. Citizenship and a British passport are a separate step.

    Source: Handbook ch. 1 · The values and principles of the UK

  10. Question 10 · True or false?

    Once you have indefinite leave to remain, you automatically become a British citizen.

    1. True
    2. False
    Show the answer

    Answer B: False

    Settlement lets you live in the UK permanently. Becoming a citizen is a separate application, usually made later.

    Source: Handbook ch. 1 · The values and principles of the UK

Worth remembering

  • The rule of law means nobody is above the law, including people in power.
  • Free speech has limits: threatening people or stirring up hatred is not protected.
  • At a citizenship ceremony you swear an oath, which mentions God, or make an affirmation, which does not. Then you make a pledge to the UK.
  • Indefinite leave to remain lets you live here permanently. Becoming a British citizen is a separate application.
