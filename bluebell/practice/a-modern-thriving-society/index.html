Life in the UK practice: Chapter 4, A modern, thriving society

Free, with no sign-up and nothing saved. These 10 questions are original, written from the facts in the handbook. No app or website can show you the real test questions.

What this chapter covers

Chapter 4 is about everyday life in the UK today: where people live, what they believe, the days they mark and what they do in their free time. It covers a wide spread of facts about all four nations, so these questions move between geography, festivals, sport, the arts and leisure.

  • The UK today: the nations and cities, languages, population and the religions practised here.
  • Customs and traditions: patron saints' days, Christmas and Easter, other festivals, bank holidays and special days.
  • Sport: the sports Britain gave the world, famous sportsmen and sportswomen, and major events.
  • Arts and culture: music, theatre, art, architecture, fashion, literature, poetry, film and comedy.
  • Leisure and places of interest: gardens, food, pastimes, national parks and landmarks.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    Roughly how far is it from John O'Groats to Land's End?

    1. 300 miles (480 km)
    2. 870 miles (1,400 km)
    3. 550 miles (885 km)
    4. 1,500 miles (2,400 km)
    Show the answer

    Answer B: 870 miles (1,400 km)

    The distance is about 870 miles, which is around 1,400 kilometres.

    Source: Handbook ch. 4 · The UK today; Religion

  2. Question 2 · Choose one answer

    Which of these cities is in Wales?

    1. Leeds
    2. Bristol
    3. Swansea
    4. Dundee
    Show the answer

    Answer C: Swansea

    Swansea is in south Wales. Dundee is in Scotland, and Bristol and Leeds are in England.

    Source: Handbook ch. 4 · The UK today; Religion

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    When is St David's Day, the patron saint's day of Wales?

    1. 23 April
    2. 1 March
    3. 17 March
    4. 30 November
    Show the answer

    Answer B: 1 March

    St David's Day is 1 March. St Patrick's is 17 March, St George's is 23 April and St Andrew's is 30 November.

    Source: Handbook ch. 4 · Customs and traditions

  4. Question 4 · Select TWO answers

    In which parts of the UK is the patron saint's day an official holiday? Select TWO.

    1. Scotland
    2. Wales
    3. England
    4. Northern Ireland
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Scotland, D: Northern Ireland

    Only Scotland and Northern Ireland treat their patron saint's day as an official holiday.

    Source: Handbook ch. 4 · Customs and traditions

  5. Question 5 · Select TWO answers

    In which of these years, apart from 2012, did London host the Olympic Games? Select TWO.

    1. 1968
    2. 1908
    3. 1936
    4. 1948
    Show the answer

    Answer B: 1908, D: 1948

    London hosted the Olympics in 1908 and 1948 as well as in 2012.

    Source: Handbook ch. 4 · Sport

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    Where did the Paralympic Games have their origins?

    1. Wembley Stadium in the north-west of London
    2. The Old Course at St Andrews
    3. Aintree Racecourse near Liverpool
    4. Stoke Mandeville Hospital, Buckinghamshire
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Stoke Mandeville Hospital, Buckinghamshire

    The Paralympics grew from sport for patients with spinal injuries at Stoke Mandeville Hospital.

    Source: Handbook ch. 4 · Sport

  9. Question 9 · Choose one answer

    What is an allotment?

    1. A type of flower show held each summer
    2. Land rented to grow fruit and vegetables

Worth remembering

  • The four patron saints' days are St David on 1 March, St Patrick on 17 March, St George on 23 April and St Andrew on 30 November.
  • Remembrance Day, 11 November, began as a way to remember those killed in the First World War. People wear red poppies and keep two minutes' silence at 11am.
  • Bonfire Night, 5 November, remembers the failure of the Gunpowder Plot of 1605.
  • Hogmanay is the Scottish name for New Year's Eve, and 2 January is also a public holiday in Scotland.
  • Diwali, the Festival of Lights, is celebrated by Hindus and Sikhs. Leicester is famous for its celebrations.
