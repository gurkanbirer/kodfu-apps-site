Free Life in the UK practice test: try 10 questions

Free, with no sign-up and nothing saved. The questions are original, written from the facts in the handbook. No app or website can show you the real test questions.

  1. Question 1 · Choose one answer

    Which principle means that the government, the police and ordinary people must all obey the same laws?

    1. Tolerance of other faiths
    2. Individual liberty
    3. Community participation
    4. The rule of law
    Show the answer

    Answer D: The rule of law

    Under the rule of law nobody is above the law, including people in power.

    Source: Handbook ch. 1 · The values and principles of the UK

  2. Question 2 · Select TWO answers

    Which of these are part of the United Kingdom? Select TWO.

    1. Wales
    2. Northern Ireland
    3. The Republic of Ireland
    4. The Isle of Man
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Wales, B: Northern Ireland

    Wales and Northern Ireland are two of the UK's four countries. The Isle of Man is a Crown dependency and Ireland is independent.

    Source: Handbook ch. 2 · What is the UK?

  3. Question 3 · Choose one answer

    In which English county is Stonehenge?

    1. Wiltshire
    2. Kent
    3. Cornwall
    4. Yorkshire
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Wiltshire

    Stonehenge stands in Wiltshire, in south-west England.

    Source: Handbook ch. 3 · Early Britain

  4. Question 4 · Choose one answer

    At which battle in 1314 did the Scots defeat the English?

    1. Bosworth Field
    2. Bannockburn
    3. Hastings
    4. Agincourt
    Show the answer

    Answer B: Bannockburn

    Robert the Bruce led the Scots to victory over the English at Bannockburn in 1314.

    Source: Handbook ch. 3 · The Middle Ages

  5. Question 5 · Select TWO answers

    Which of Henry VIII's wives were executed? Select TWO.

    1. Anne Boleyn
    2. Catherine Parr
    3. Catherine Howard
    4. Jane Seymour
    Show the answer

    Answer A: Anne Boleyn, C: Catherine Howard

    Anne Boleyn and her cousin Catherine Howard were both executed. Jane Seymour died after childbirth and Catherine Parr outlived Henry.

    Source: Handbook ch. 3 · The Tudors and Stuarts

  6. Question 6 · Choose one answer

    Why did George I rely heavily on his ministers?

    1. He lived in France
    2. He spoke little English
    3. He was too young to rule
    4. He was often ill
    Show the answer

    Answer B: He spoke little English

    George I spoke little English, so his ministers took on more of the work of government.

    Source: Handbook ch. 3 · A global power

  7. Question 7 · Choose one answer

    Which Minister for Health led the setting up of the NHS?

    1. William Beveridge
    2. R A Butler
    3. Clement Attlee
    4. Aneurin (Nye) Bevan
    Show the answer

    Answer D: Aneurin (Nye) Bevan

    Aneurin Bevan, Minister for Health, led the creation of the NHS in 1948.

    Source: Handbook ch. 3 · Britain since 1945

  8. Question 8 · Choose one answer

    Which flower is associated with Wales?

    1. The rose
    2. The shamrock
    3. The daffodil
    4. The thistle
    Show the answer

    Answer C: The daffodil

    The daffodil is the flower of Wales. The thistle is Scotland's, the rose England's and the shamrock Northern Ireland's.

    Source: Handbook ch. 4 · Leisure; Places of interest

  9. Question 9 · Choose one answer

    Which statement about British democracy is correct?

    1. It developed gradually over many years
    2. It was introduced all at once after a revolution
    3. It was copied from the French constitution
    4. It began only after the Second World War
    Show the answer

    Answer A: It developed gradually over many years

    Britain's democracy grew step by step, as the right to vote was gradually widened.

    Source: Handbook ch. 5 · British democracy and the government

  10. Question 10 · True or false?

    Civil servants are politically neutral and are chosen on merit.

    1. True
    2. False
    Show the answer

    Answer A: True

    Civil servants are not political appointees. They are selected on merit and must stay neutral.

    Source: Handbook ch. 5 · Devolution; The UK and the world
