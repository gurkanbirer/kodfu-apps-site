Life in the UK Test guide: format, booking, ID and the day itself

A plain-English summary of what GOV.UK says about the test, read on 09/11/2026. Rules can change, so check GOV.UK before you book or apply.

Do you need the test?

You may need to pass it as part of an application for British citizenship or settlement in the UK. The guidance and form for your application say whether you do.

GOV.UK says you do not need to take it if you:

  • are under 18
  • are 65 or over
  • have passed it before, for example for an earlier settlement application
  • have a long-term physical or mental condition, shown by an exemption form or a letter from a doctor

The test format

  • 24 questions in 45 minutes.
  • You need 75% or more to pass, which is 18 of the 24.
  • You are only tested on the handbook, Life in the United Kingdom: A Guide for New Residents. GOV.UK says to study it to prepare.
  • The handbook comes as a book, an eBook or in audio formats, from the shop linked from GOV.UK.

Source: What happens at the test (GOV.UK)

Booking

  • Book online at least 3 days in advance. It costs £50.
  • GOV.UK links to the only government booking service for the test: book the test.
  • There are over 30 test centres in the UK, and you choose one when you book.
  • You need an email address, a debit or credit card and an accepted form of ID.
  • The name on your booking must exactly match the name on the ID you use to book.
  • You can make special requests when you book, for example if you have a disability and need extra equipment or help getting into the centre.

If you have changed gender and do not want test centre staff to see your previous gender, GOV.UK asks you to email the Home Office before booking. The address is on the GOV.UK booking page.

What ID to bring

If you have an eVisa, GOV.UK says you should use a share code to book. You get one by signing in to your UK Visas and Immigration account: view your eVisa and get a share code.

If you do not have an eVisa or cannot access it, you can use one of these:

  • a valid passport
  • a valid ID card from the EU, Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein or Norway
  • a valid travel document with a photo (not an emergency travel document)
  • a biometric residence permit or card, as long as your test is no later than 24 months after the expiry date printed on the card or before 31 December 2026, whichever comes first

The ID must have a photo that looks like you. If you have none of these, contact UK Visas and Immigration.

On the day you must bring the same ID you used to book. It must be the original, not a copy or a screenshot.

What happens on the day

  • You cannot bring children or other family members with you to the centre.
  • Your photo is taken on the day to confirm your ID.
  • If you do not bring the correct ID, or you refuse to have your photo taken, you will not be able to take the test and you will not get a refund.
  • You then have 45 minutes to answer the 24 questions.

If you pass

You get a unique reference number. You need it to complete your citizenship or settlement application, and the Home Office uses it to check that you passed.

If you do not pass

You can rebook as many times as you need, and you pay each time.

Cancelling or rearranging

  • You get a refund if you cancel at least 3 days (72 hours) before your test. You will not get one if you cancel or rearrange within 3 days of it.
  • To cancel, sign in to your Life in the UK account, select Confirmed tests, then Cancel tests. The £50 goes back to the card you booked with.
  • You can ask for a refund if the test centre cancels the test, within 3 months of the test date.
  • You cannot ask for a refund if you brought the wrong ID or documents, were ill, were late, or refused to have your photo taken.

Source: Cancellations, refunds and complaints (GOV.UK)
